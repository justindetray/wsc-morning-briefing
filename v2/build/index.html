THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Markets are bouncing this morning after tech's worst week since June: Nasdaq futures are up about 1%, led by the same chip stocks that fell hardest. One good morning does not settle the AI financing question, so the right response to last week is the same as the right response to today: rebalance, don't chase or flee. Oil has eased a bit after the White House waived Russia sanctions, China's growth beat expectations, and this week's earnings from Netflix, Meta and Tesla will tell us more than headlines. Long-term plans do not need to change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is a rebound after a rough week, not a change in direction either way.

Where we are: the S&P 500 is about 2% below its October 6 record close after falling 1.95% last week; the Nasdaq 100 fell 5.5%; the Dow rose. This morning S&P futures are up about 0.6% and Nasdaq futures about 1.2%, led by Intel, Micron and other chip makers.

The AI question is still open. Goldman's traders say AI-related credit spreads are the widest of the year, and CoreWeave's default insurance is priced at distress levels. Money is still flowing to AI, but lenders are asking for more, and the weakest borrowers are being sorted out. That is a credit story inside a healthy demand story, and it can take weeks to play out.

Oil: Brent is about $108, down from Friday's five-month high, after the White House suspended Russia sanctions and waived tariffs on buyers of Russian oil to bring fuel prices down. Hormuz attacks remain elevated and there is no deal with Iran, so oil is still the main inflation risk and the reason markets price about an 85% chance of a December Fed hike.

Elsewhere: China grew 4.5%, a bit better than expected, and riots in France knocked European stocks lower this morning.

Why it matters: diversified portfolios handled last week as designed. Bonds paying about 5% cushioned the tech drop, and the Dow rose. Sharp one-week drops in leadership stocks happen inside long bull markets, and selling into them has historically cost more than it saved.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and do not read too much into one up morning or one down week. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Friday 5.09%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Friday 5.43%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $108.45). Brent is already there; the missing piece is strikes.
