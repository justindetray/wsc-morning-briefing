THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

Start with what actually happened, because the headline and the tape disagreed for the better part of an hour. Nvidia reported revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year over year, with data-centre revenue of $89 billion, up 117%, and guided the current quarter to $108 billion plus or minus 2% against roughly $104.2 billion expected. Those figures are from the company's own release. And the stock went down. Reuters is the only source that reconciles the sequence: shares opened the after-hours session lower, were little changed for about 40 minutes, and the mood picked up with the conference call, after which Nvidia rose 4.2% on more than 50 million shares. What changed inside those 40 minutes was not a number from the quarter just ended - it was management putting roughly 70% revenue growth for fiscal 2028 on the call. By this morning the stock is quoted between $223.62 and $225.21, up 6.7% to 7.4% depending on the snapshot, and Swissquote's Ipek Ozkardeskaya's line captures the mood: no matter how high the expectations were, Nvidia managed to beat all of them.

The part I would actually raise with a client is the margin, not the revenue. Nvidia guided gross margin DOWN - 74.0% this quarter, troughing at 71% to 72% in the fourth quarter, on rising memory costs. On the very same evening, HP Inc. beat revenue by $1.24 billion and fell roughly 9% overnight after its CFO flagged below-seasonal fourth-quarter revenue on commodity-driven price increases and rising memory costs. Two companies at opposite ends of the technology stack naming the identical cause. That is a hardware cost cycle turning, not a demand problem, and it is a more durable fact than any single quarter's revenue beat. The wider pattern from last night is worth naming plainly: of four large after-close reporters, three beat and two were sold. Salesforce rose about 11% and CrowdStrike about 9%, while Synopsys beat cleanly and fell 1.3% and HP beat enormously and was punished. The print is not the risk; the guide and the margin path are.

Now the uncomfortable half. While equities celebrated a forward narrative, the interest-rate market moved the other way, and it moved uniformly. Our own CME cohort refreshed to Wednesday's settle and cumulative hike odds ROSE at every single meeting - 39.6% for September, 54.3% by October, 73.5% by December and 78.2% by January - with the probability of a CUT still exactly zero at every meeting through January 2027. The cause was yesterday morning's data. Core PCE held at 3.3% year over year, which Reuters notes is the 65th consecutive month above the Fed's 2% target, and the unrounded monthly figure was 0.246%, a whisker below the rounding boundary. Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid put it best: while July core PCE came in line with consensus, the details of the release were more inflationary. Underneath a headline second-quarter GDP figure of just 1.5%, real final sales to private domestic purchasers ran at 4.2%, the strongest in over three years. Soft headline growth, hot private demand, sticky inflation, and a labour market near cycle lows on claims. Boston's Susan Collins said on Tuesday it may be appropriate to tighten policy soon, and she specifically noted the AI build-out appears to be putting upward pressure on core goods inflation - which ties the two halves of this brief together rather neatly.

Two live cautions and one date. Crude fell a fourth session for Brent and a fifth for WTI, to roughly $86.48 and $80.83 on the Reuters oil desk, because Qatar's Prime Minister travels to Tehran TODAY to relaunch Hormuz mediation. Cheaper oil is genuine disinflation arriving from diplomacy rather than from Washington, but it is reversible by a single incident - a tanker was struck by a projectile near Khasab, Oman, inside the same overnight window, and Treasury's promised designation of a large foreign financial institution still has NOT happened with Bessent's stated deadline landing this week. I will also flag a data disagreement honestly rather than paper over it: TradingEconomics had crude UP and about $1.80 higher this morning, against two separate Reuters snapshots showing it down, so we shipped the wire and logged the other feed as degraded. The date that matters is tomorrow at 10:00, when Chair Warsh gives his first Jackson Hole address; the symposium agenda does not even publish until 8:00 tonight. For clients: last night was a reminder that the AI complex is now priced off forward narrative rather than delivered results, and the semiconductor index still sits 20.7% below its own June record even after a 106% revenue quarter from its largest member - which is precisely why we size positions to a plan rather than to a print.
