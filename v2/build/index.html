THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Friday’s selling spread beyond technology, but chip stocks still took the hardest hit. Buyers came back to chips and the Nasdaq tracker after the close; a bounce in thin trading is not yet a recovery.

If you have cash set aside for upcoming spending, Friday’s prices need not decide what you sell. What matters next is whether buyers also show up for the broader market when regular trading resumes.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

On Friday the S&P 500 fell 3.70%, the Nasdaq-100 fell 5.61% and the semiconductor index fell 10.73%. That answers the question we had before the open: losses did spread, but they were far from evenly distributed. After the close, chip shares recovered only part of their decline.

Consider what the two percentages buy. The semiconductor ETF closed at $123.40 after starting from a previous close of $137.95. The extended-session quote of $126.50 was better, but someone who held through both sessions was still well below Thursday’s close. Neither print tells us how much stock could change hands at that price on Monday.

Households offered a different perspective. The Michigan survey showed a decline in expected inflation, but interviews ended on November 16. It is not a poll of how families felt after Friday’s losses. Nor does a rise in the volatility index tell us sellers have finished.

For clients: start with what the money is for. Near-term withdrawals and a concentrated chip position deserve a different conversation from a diversified retirement account with years to invest. When trading resumes, watch whether the recovery extends beyond the names that fell furthest.
