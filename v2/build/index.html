Overnight

  • THE TEN-YEAR BROKE 5% OVERNIGHT AND HELD IT. Monday's poke above 5% reversed into the cash close - Treasury's own CMT settled the 10-year at 4.97% and the 2-year at 4.65%, both a basis point or two higher on the day. Overnight the long end went straight back through: 5.025% at 1:10 a.m. ET, with the 30-year at 5.384% and the 2-year at 4.68%. IG's Sydney desk independently logged it above 5.02% in the Asian session, the highest since 2007. One caution that survives from Monday: the 30-year TIES rather than exceeds the June 2007 level, so it is 'highest since', not 'highest ever'.
  • FED FUNDS FUTURES NOW PRICE A 93.5% CHANCE OF A HIKE TOMORROW AND A 0.0% CHANCE OF A CUT. Straight from the CME feed at Monday's settle: hold 6.5%, +25bp 93.5%. That is up from 87.5% at Friday's settle. Further out the modal path has hardened - October is a coin flip between one and two hikes (53.0% / 43.5%), and by the December meeting the single most likely cumulative outcome is +50bp at 49.9%, with +75bp at 29.1%. There is no cut priced at any meeting through March 2027. Reuters' 9 September economist poll, which had 65 of 93 forecasters at hold, was superseded by a fresh Reuters poll dated 14 September headlined that a hike is now likely with at least one more to follow.
  • THE SAUDI PIPELINE IS A THREE-TO-FIVE WEEK REPAIR, NOT A FEW DAYS. AP, published Tuesday and citing two regional officials, puts repairs to the East-West line - including a major pumping facility - at three to five weeks, with partial operation possible during the work. Yanbu's 5-7 days of export cover is therefore materially shorter than the outage. There is still no restart, and critically no official Saudi word at all: Aramco did not respond to AP and the Saudi Centre for International Communication said it was 'looking into' the inquiry. Crude is up but orderly - WTI 102.87 (+1.46%), Brent 106.56 (+0.83%). Separately the Houthis captured the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands Monday, about 160km north of Bab el-Mandeb.
  • SEMIS ARE IN A BEAR MARKET WHILE THE INDEX IS BARELY OFF ITS HIGH. Monday's cash session was not a broad risk event: the S&P 500 fell 0.48% to 7,619.98 and the Dow 0.29%, but the Philadelphia Semiconductor index fell 5.86% to 11,131.28 - it opened 5.3% below Friday's close, so the damage was a gap, not a grind. That leaves SOX 23.9% below its 22 June record close of 14,634.72 while the S&P 500 sits 2.3% below its 13 August record of 7,798.99 and the Nasdaq 100 5.0% below its 2 June record of 30,660.60. None of the four is at a record on either the close or the intraday-high leg. The VIX rose 7.95% to 17.10.
  • THE AI UNWIND IS NOT EXTENDING THIS MORNING. THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Two things are worth saying out loud this morning. First, the bond market has stopped waiting for the Fed - the 10-year traded through 5% overnight and stayed there, and futures now put a rate HIKE tomorrow at roughly 93%, with no cut priced at any meeting into 2027. That is a genuine regime change from where we were in the spring, and it is why the long end, not the equity tape, is the thing to watch this week. Second, the scary-looking selling has been remarkably narrow. Semiconductors are down about 24% from their June high while the S&P 500 is only about 2% off its own record - that is a rotation out of one very crowded theme, not a market-wide repricing. Owning a diversified portfolio is doing exactly what it is supposed to do right now.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

The honest summary of this morning is that the interest-rate story has finally overtaken the artificial-intelligence story, and that is a healthier problem to have.

Start with rates, because everything else hangs off them. The US 10-year Treasury yield went through 5% overnight and held above it - 5.025% at around 1 a.m. eastern, the highest since 2007 - after Treasury's official curve settled it at 4.97% Monday afternoon. The 30-year is at roughly 5.38%, which ties rather than exceeds its June 2007 level. More striking than the level is the expectation: fed funds futures taken straight off the CME's own feed put the odds of a quarter-point rate INCREASE at tomorrow's meeting at 93.5%, with a 0.0% chance of a cut at that meeting or at any meeting through March 2027. By December the single most likely outcome is that the Fed has raised by a cumulative half point. A Reuters poll on 9 September still had two-thirds of economists expecting no change; a fresh Reuters poll dated 14 September flipped to a hike being likely, with at least one more to follow. When the professional consensus moves that far in five days, it is usually because the data moved, not because the commentary did.

What moved the data is energy. The Saudi East-West pipeline, shut after a drone attack launched from Iraq, is now reported to be a three-to-five week repair rather than a few-day outage, and there has still been no official statement from Aramco or the Saudi energy ministry. Crude is up but orderly. The place it is showing up hardest is diesel: the US national average set a ninth consecutive record this morning at $6.2694 a gallon, up 70% from a year ago. Diesel is the fuel of freight, farming and construction, so it feeds into the price of almost everything with a delay of a month or two. That is the mechanism behind a Fed that is now expected to tighten rather than ease.

Now the part that actually matters for a portfolio. Monday looked ugly in the headlines, but the damage was extraordinarily concentrated. The S&P 500 fell less than half a percent and the Dow less than a third of a percent, while the semiconductor index fell 5.9% in a single session. Step back and the gap is wider still: semis are about 24% below their June record, the Nasdaq 100 about 5% below its June record, and the S&P 500 only about 2% below the record it set in mid-August. This is a crowded theme being repriced, not a market being repriced. It is also precisely the scenario diversification is built for - the pain is real if you owned only the one story, and comparatively mild if you did not.

The trigger for the semiconductor selling is worth understanding because it is unusual. It was not an earnings miss or a demand signal. It was a public argument among AI company leaders themselves - Anthropic's chief executive published an essay on Saturday urging the industry to slow down, Elon Musk and Sam Altman both agreed, and Altman separately ruled out an OpenAI public offering this year. Markets reacted to the possibility that the spending cycle gets deliberately paced rather than accelerating indefinitely. This morning that reaction is already fading - SoftBank recovered about 8% in Tokyo and Nvidia is slightly higher pre-market. Narrative-driven moves tend to mean-revert; spending cycles take quarters to actually change.

What to watch, in order. Tomorrow at 2 p.m. eastern the Fed announces, and the projections and dot plot matter far more than the decision itself, which is essentially fully priced. This afternoon at 1 p.m. Treasury reopens $13 billion of 20-year bonds, which is a clean real-time test of whether investors will fund a tightening Fed at these yields. Friday brings the Bank of Japan and quarterly options expiry. And Canada's 50% tariff scope change went live at 12:01 this morning with no official reaction yet.

The thing that would change this view is not a bad day in semiconductors. It would be a failed 20-year auction or a disorderly move above 5.25% on the 10-year, because that is the point at which higher rates stop being a story about a strong economy and start being a story about who is willing to lend.
