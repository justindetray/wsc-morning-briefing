THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Today is about inflation. September's consumer price report at 8:30 is expected to show prices up about 3.7% from a year ago, mostly because of energy, with core inflation near 2.5%. The banks told the more useful story yesterday: profits are strong, but rates near 5% are starting to bite, and Jamie Dimon warned that a move to 6% would hurt more than the move to 5% did. Stocks are steady, 0.17% below their record. For clients: a hot headline number is mostly energy, and the long-term plan does not change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is the inflation report, read through what the banks said yesterday.

The report: at 8:30 AM the government publishes September consumer prices. Forecasters expect 3.7% over the past year, up from 3.4%, driven mainly by gasoline and energy. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, is expected around 2.5%. That gap is the point: most of the increase is oil, not a broad wage-price spiral.

The banks: the four biggest reporters showed strong profits, led by Goldman, which earned $17.01 a share against $13.02 expected. Even so, all four stocks fell, because management warned that higher rates and sticky inflation are starting to bite. Jamie Dimon said a move in rates from 5% to 6% would be more painful than the move from 4% to 5%, while saying clearly he is not predicting it.

The Fed: New York Fed President Williams still sees one more hike this year but not more in 2027. Futures put an October hike near 22%.

Oil: Brent is about $106. Iranian officials told Reuters they are stepping up attacks on shipping to keep prices high before the US midterm elections. That is the inflation story in one sentence.

Chips: ASML, the maker of the machines that make advanced chips, said it cannot fully meet demand through 2028, a useful counterweight to this week's AI funding scare.

Why it matters: higher-for-longer rates are good news for savers and bond buyers and a headwind for borrowers and richly valued stocks. Diversified portfolios are built for this mix.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds pay more than 5% for a decade, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Tuesday 5.21%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Tuesday 5.56%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $106.56).
