THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The Fed story got a little softer yesterday, and the bond story did not. Traders cut the odds of an October rate hike from about 70% to under 50% after a senior Fed official said there is no rush. But long-term Treasury yields still rose, so borrowing costs for mortgages and businesses are not getting relief from a gentler Fed. Stocks barely moved, and chip stocks rose. Today's inflation report is the real test; nothing here calls for action before we see it, and yields near 5% remain an opportunity for idle cash.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, there are two headlines that seem to conflict, and explaining why they do not is the useful part.

First, the Fed. On Monday markets saw about a 70% chance of another rate hike in October. By Tuesday afternoon it was under 50%. New York Fed President Williams said there is no urgency and the Fed has time to gather data, and consumer confidence dropped to its lowest since 2014. A softer path for the Fed pulled the 2-year Treasury yield down. Job openings were steady at 7.1 million, so this is not a labor market cracking; it is a Fed signaling patience after already hiking in September.

Second, long rates. The 10-year Treasury still rose to 5.26%, matching its June 2007 close, and the 30-year reached levels last seen in 2002. That is investors demanding more to lend for a long time: large deficits, sticky inflation and heavy borrowing to fund AI data centers. Mortgage and corporate borrowing costs key off these long rates, so relief from the Fed is not reaching them.

We said a 10-year close above 5.25% would change our view. It closed at 5.26%, so it is worth being precise about what changes. It raises the bar for rate-sensitive stocks and real estate, and it strengthens the case for locking in longer bond yields for clients who need income, since those yields are the best on offer in almost two decades. It does not change the equity plan: the S&P 500 is 1.64% below its August 13 record, and stocks fell less than 0.2% on the day, with semiconductors up more than 1%. Diversified portfolios built for a higher-rate world are doing their job.

The next test is this morning's PCE inflation report, expected at 3.7% year over year. What would change the view further: a hot PCE print that pushes October odds back above 70%, a 10-year close above 5.40%, or the 30-year holding above 5.75%.
