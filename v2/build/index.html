THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The story this morning is interest rates, not headlines. The 10-year Treasury closed at 5.24% yesterday, its highest close since 2007, and stocks slipped less than 1%. The whole rise was in real yields, meaning investors are demanding more return for lending, not bracing for more inflation. Futures are flat this morning, and oil is firm but off its overnight highs. Nothing here calls for action; this is the best yield on safe money in nearly two decades, and Wednesday's inflation report and Friday's jobs report matter more than today.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the headline they will have seen is that bond yields are at their highest since 2007. That is true, and it is the single most useful thing to explain today.

The 10-year Treasury closed at 5.24% on Monday, its highest close since June 2007. The 2-year rose even more, to 4.92%, because the market now sees about a 70% chance the Fed raises rates again on October 28 and almost certainly by December. Central banks are moving together: Australia raised rates overnight to a 15-year high, also citing energy costs.

The part worth slowing down on is why yields rose. Inflation-protected Treasury yields rose by the same amount, so the market's inflation expectation did not change. Investors are simply demanding a higher real return to lend money. That is a tighter financial environment, but it is not a panic signal, and it is not the market saying inflation is running away. It also matters for the mortgage and borrowing conversations clients are having: longer-term borrowing costs are set by these yields, so rates on new loans are likely to stay elevated until the Fed signals it is done.

Stocks are handling it in stride. The S&P 500 fell 0.77% Monday and is 1.48% below its August 13 record; futures are flat this morning. Oil is firm on the Iran standoff, with Iran awaiting a formal US answer through Qatar today, but it has not driven a new leg down in stocks.

For clients, the practical point is that cash and high-quality bonds are paying their best real returns in almost two decades. That is an opportunity for idle money and for anyone who needs income, not a reason to sell equities built for the long run. Locking in some of today's yields for several years is a reasonable conversation for clients with near-term spending needs. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.25% (we closed at 5.24%), a hot August PCE report on Wednesday, or Brent holding above $110.
