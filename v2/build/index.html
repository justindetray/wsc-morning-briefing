THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The weekend passed without a strike, and markets are calmer this morning: oil is down about 2% on reports of mediation, and stock futures are slightly higher. Trump says it is "decision time" between a deal and finishing the war, and he has pointed to after the November 3 midterms, so the oil risk is postponed, not gone. This week the Fed, Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon will matter more for portfolios than any single headline. For clients: stay diversified and rebalanced; plans do not need to change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is a calmer start to a very busy week, with the Iran decision hanging over oil.

What changed over the weekend: no US strike. Trump said there would be no strike Sunday, called it decision time between a deal and finishing it off, and has pointed to after the November 3 midterms for any renewed attacks. Qatari mediators visited Tehran, and Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish defense chiefs meet Iran's leaders today. Brent eased about 2% to $115.

Where we stand: the S&P 500 is about 2.5% below its October 6 record close after two down weeks. The Nasdaq 100 has been hit harder by AI spending worries, while the Dow has held up. Bonds rallied last week, with the 10-year back to 5.00%.

This week: the Fed decides Wednesday and is expected to hold; markets still lean toward a hike in December. Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday and Amazon Thursday, alongside GDP and PCE inflation. Those reports are the real tests of the AI spending story. About a quarter of S&P 500 companies have reported so far, and analysts describe results as healthy, with resilient demand.

Elsewhere: Brazilian stocks are down sharply after an upset election result, a reminder that emerging-market positions can move a lot on politics and why position sizing matters.

Why it matters: the biggest risks are scheduled (earnings, the Fed) or postponed (Iran), which gives investors time to rebalance calmly rather than react.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and keep bonds working at today's yields. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Friday 5.00%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Friday 5.33%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $114.94). Brent is there; strikes are the missing piece, and the timing now points after November 3.
