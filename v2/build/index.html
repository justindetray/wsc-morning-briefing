Runs #145–146 carried approximate WTI ~$72–73 / Brent ~$77; the verified Friday settles were WTI $78.18 and Brent $83.55. That is a ~$6 miss and it understated the energy-inflation risk running into Wednesday's CPI. Both benchmarks are now on wire-verified marks and are up again this morning (WTI $78.80 +0.8%, Brent $84.40 +1.0%) — though still −7–8% on the week. Second, Iran hardened rather than softened. Araqchi says the shipping-lane framework is in 'final stages' but attached a list of preconditions — sanctions relief, war-damage compensation, lifting the naval blockade, frozen-asset release — and the IRGC said flatly there is no reopening until all of them are met. There are still no direct talks and no signature. Hormuz traffic remains roughly 33 vessels/week against a 130–140 pre-war baseline (Reuters; Lloyd's List disputes with 84 — unresolved). The market spent last week trading the deal headline; the tape is now trading the conditions, which is why Dow futures are underperforming this morning. Third, Bowman spoke Saturday, not today — and the widely-circulated claim that she endorsed three cuts starting in September traces only to low-trust retail feeds with no transcript. Do not position on it. There are no Fed speakers Monday through Wednesday; Hammack and Barkin land Thursday, after CPI. What actually matters this week is a 10-basis-point disagreement. Core CPI MoM consensus is genuinely split — MarketWatch has +0.3%, Reuters/JPMorgan have +0.2%. Feroli's framing is the useful one: +0.2% probably does not justify a September move, +0.3% does. With Sept currently priced hold-favored (~56–60%) off a −23k payroll print, a +0.3% core forces the market to hold two contradictory ideas at once — deteriorating labor and sticky inflation — which is the exact mix Warsh has flagged as the hard case. Positioning read: VIX at 14.90 is not pricing a two-sided CPI with unresolved Hormuz supply risk on top. That is the cheapest part of the tape. Equities carry a genuine ATH close in SPX (7,757.64, verified) but NDX is still −3.1% under its June high — this is a recovery tape, not price discovery. Berkshire's net-equity-buy after a multi-year drought is a real signal but it is a quarterly-lag signal, not a Monday catalyst, and its premarket reaction is genuinely unconfirmed this morning. Do not add directional risk ahead of Wednesday; own optionality instead of opinion.
