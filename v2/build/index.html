CLIENT FRAME
Two stories worth separating. The S&P 500 set a genuine new all-time high Thursday, closing 7,798.99 — above the prior record close of 7,757.64 from August 7 — with an intraday high of 7,816.70 that also cleared the August 5 peak of 7,793.68. Both verified against daily history. To be precise about scope: this is an S&P record only. The Nasdaq 100 reclaimed 30,000 at 30,084.50 but sits 1.88% below its June 2 peak, and the Dow is 0.94% below its August 5 high. September hike odds fell to hold 65.9% / hike 34.1%, the third straight decline from 49.6% hike on Monday. The reason for the rally was headline PPI at 0.0% MoM against +0.2% expected, with the YoY rate dropping to 4.7% from 5.5%. But the print was two-sided, and the part that matters for policy went the other way: core ex-food, energy and trade services rose +0.4% MoM, up from +0.1% in June. Driving it was a 5.6–6.5% monthly surge in portfolio management services prices — which is worth sitting with, because those prices largely track asset values. The market rally is mechanically feeding measured inflation. Pantheon lifted its July core PCE estimate to +0.24% from +0.16%, attributing roughly 11bp to that single component, and expects the BEA's September 30 methodology revision to subtract at least 0.2pp from core PCE year-over-year. So the near-term data looks firmer than the headline while a scheduled statistical change points the other way. Neither is really about underlying inflation. Meanwhile the long end kept telling a different story than stocks. Thursday's $25B 30-year auction cleared at 5.216%, the highest in over 15 years, with bid-to-cover slipping to 2.39x from 2.44x and primary dealers absorbing 11.5% versus a 10.6% average. Coupled with Wednesday's 10-year at a post-2007 high yield, that is two consecutive auctions of softening demand — real deterioration, though neither produced a large tail. And overnight the UAE accused Iran of attacking two ADNOC tankers in Hormuz, ADNOC's 15th and 16th vessels struck since February, sending Brent back up 1.7%. Monday is the reported expiry of the 60-day negotiating window.
