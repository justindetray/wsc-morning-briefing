THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The weekend tilted slightly toward diplomacy, but both paths stay open. Iran says a plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days is on the table if its conditions are met, and Washington says no strike decision has been made. At the same time, reports still point to possible US strikes as early as Wednesday, and Iran's leadership is issuing harder warnings, so oil can swing either way when futures reopen Sunday evening. For clients: this is a week of headlines (Iran, the midterms, Apple, and the jobs report). Stay diversified and keep to the plan.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this weekend, the honest answer is that the next few days could move markets in either direction, and that is exactly why we do not trade on headlines.

What changed: Iran's foreign minister said Qatari mediation shortened its plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz from five days to two, provided the US meets conditions such as sanctions relief and releasing frozen funds. Secretary of State Rubio said President Trump has not decided on new strikes and prefers diplomacy. Trump said talks are going fine but that one or two targets, including a suspected nuclear site, remain. Iran's supreme leader ordered the military to respond to any attack without geographic limits.

Where we stand: on Friday the S&P 500 fell 1.8% and the Nasdaq 2.9% on Intel and Amazon. For the week the S&P fell about 0.6% and is about 3% below its October 6 record close. Brent settled at $113.10 and gold at $3,898.90. The 10-year Treasury yield is 4.94%.

The week ahead: Apple and Palantir report Monday, the midterm elections are Tuesday, and the October jobs report is Friday, with economists expecting about 65,000 new jobs after just 29,000 in September. Reports say strikes could come as early as Wednesday.

Why it matters: a credible Hormuz reopening would likely pull oil sharply lower and ease inflation and rate-hike fears; renewed strikes would push oil higher and add to inflation worries. A diversified portfolio is built to live through both outcomes without needing to guess which one comes first, or when.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and avoid making election-week or Iran-headline trades in either direction. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Friday 4.94%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Friday 5.31%), or US strikes that keep Brent well above $105 (Friday $113.10).
