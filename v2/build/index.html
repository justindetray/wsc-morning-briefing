THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Stocks rallied Monday on excitement over Anthropic's IPO filing, and futures are flat this morning as the Fed begins its two-day meeting. Iran held talks with Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan but no deal emerged, so oil is still near $115 and the risk is on hold rather than resolved. The Fed Wednesday and Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon later this week matter more for portfolios than any single headline. For clients: stay diversified, rebalanced and patient; with yields near 5%, bonds are paid to wait, and plans do not need to change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is a good Monday for tech, a quiet night, and a Fed meeting that starts today.

What changed overnight: Iran met senior Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials in Tehran. All four agreed to keep talking, but Iran says it will not deal while the US naval blockade stays in place, and it calls Washington not serious. Trump has said there will be no strikes before the November 3 midterms. Oil swung in a $2.40 range overnight and sits near $115 Brent. Asian stocks rose strongly; European stocks dipped on a disappointing HSBC report.

Where we stand: the S&P 500 rose 0.7% Monday and chip stocks 2.8%, helped by Anthropic's IPO filing, which showed very fast revenue growth alongside heavy spending. The S&P 500 is now about 1.8% below its October 6 record close. The 10-year Treasury yield is steady at 5.00%.

This week: the Fed decides Wednesday and is expected to hold, with markets leaning toward a December hike. Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday and Amazon Thursday, alongside GDP and PCE inflation. Today brings Coca-Cola, Boeing, UPS and Visa, a useful read on the consumer and on travel and shipping costs with oil this high.

Elsewhere: Brazilian stocks fell almost 14% Monday and the currency about 8% after the election upset, a reminder that single-country emerging-market positions can move a lot on politics and why position sizing matters.

Why it matters: the AI spending debate swung back toward optimism in one day, which shows how quickly sentiment can turn. The biggest risks this week are scheduled (earnings, the Fed) or postponed (Iran), which gives investors time to rebalance calmly rather than react.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and keep bonds working at today's yields. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Monday 5.00%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Monday 5.31%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $114.99). Brent is there; strikes are the missing piece, and the timing still points after November 3.
