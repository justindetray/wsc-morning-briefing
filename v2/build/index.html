THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the short version is that the election sparked a broad relief rally, but the leadership underneath is changing.

What happened: Wednesday the Dow jumped 3%, its best day of 2026, and the S&P 500 rose 1.6% to about 1.7% below its October 6 record close. Oil fell about 8%, with Brent near $95, as investors bet that a Democratic Congress makes a negotiated end to the Iran war more likely. Volatility dropped sharply, with the VIX at 16.6. At the same time, chip stocks fell about 3% as investors worried about tighter rules for AI data centers.

What changed overnight: oil bounced about 1.5% after Iran said two tankers and a tugboat caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz. Trump said the war largely cost Republicans the election but that he would do it again, and Iran's supreme leader said the vote changes nothing. Stock futures are mixed, with the Nasdaq 100 down 0.5%.

Where we stand: the 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.90%. Futures markets now see an 83% chance of a December Fed hike, and the Bank of England is expected to raise rates this morning. The October jobs report comes tomorrow.

Why it matters: lower oil is helping, but central banks are still leaning toward tightening, and the market is rotating away from the most crowded AI names toward a broader set of companies. That favors diversified portfolios over concentrated tech bets. Divided government also tends to mean fewer surprise policy changes, which markets have usually welcomed over time.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and avoid chasing Wednesday's rally or selling into the AI pullback. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Wednesday 4.90%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Wednesday 5.28%), or renewed US strikes that send Brent back well above $105 (now $96.06).
