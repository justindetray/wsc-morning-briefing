Overnight

  • SAUDI ARABIA SHUT ITS EAST-WEST PIPELINE AFTER DRONE STRIKES LAUNCHED FROM IRAQ. The 1,200-km line across the Arabian Peninsula - the designated workaround for a throttled Strait of Hormuz - was hit Thursday morning in the Riyadh and Medina regions and shut as a precaution. Reuters filed at 00:55 UTC Saturday (20:55 ET Friday), inside the overnight window. Report both capacity figures and label them: nameplate is about 7mn b/d (Argus), while recent throughput was 4-5mn b/d, roughly 4-5% of global supply (Reuters). Riyadh said it will not retaliate for now, at the Iraqi prime minister's request, while reserving the right to act.
  • THE HOUTHIS COMPLETED CONTROL OF BAB EL-MANDEB BY SEIZING PERIM ISLAND. Mayyun (Perim) Island sits mid-strait and splits the narrowest point into two shipping lanes. Dhubab and Murad fell the same day, putting the whole of Yemen's Red Sea coast under Houthi control in an 18-hour advance; Mokha and Zuqar Island were taken Thursday. Their military spokesman said Friday evening that navigation is safe "for all companies except Saudi ships, which have already been banned." With Hormuz throttled and the Saudi bypass down, the Red Sea exit of that bypass is the leg that just changed hands.
  • DIESEL SET A SIXTH STRAIGHT RECORD OVERNIGHT: $6.1602 A GALLON. AAA's own highest-recorded-average row is now date-stamped 9/12/26 with today's value, which is the cleanest possible confirmation. Diesel rose 10.46c in a single day against 1.54c for regular gasoline - a seven-to-one split - and is up 66.4% year over year versus 35.2% for gasoline. Regular at $4.3104 is still 70.6c below its June 2022 record of $5.0165. California: diesel $8.0956, regular $5.9732.
  • UKMTO POSTED ITS 2026 RUNNING TALLY THIS MORNING: 83 IRAN-US CONFLICT INCIDENTS AND 39 PIRACY ATTACKS OR HIJACKINGS. That is the count across Middle East shipping lanes so far this year. Hormuz transit counts themselves are unreconciled across vendors using different vessel-class definitions - one puts it at three vessels, another at seven - so the honest statement is that traffic is down to single digits, not a specific number.
  • BITCOIN IS THE ONLY MARKET OPEN AND IT IS GOING NOWHERE. At $77,309 it is up 0.13% from Friday's UTC close of $77,208.55, and note what that means - crypto sat out Friday's risk-on entirely and is down about 3% on the week while the S&P was recovering. Friday itself was a leverage flush that repaired rather than a trend move: a range worth 5% of price that closed back near its open, with ether outperforming on short liquidations. A 25bp hike Wednesday is not being priced as friendly here.
THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

August inflation came in hot where it counts least and cool where it counts most. Headline was +0.4% for the month and 3.4% over the year, both as expected, but core - stripping out food and energy - ran +0.3% against +0.2% expected. Here is the part worth knowing: essentially the entire overshoot is fuel working its way through the economy, not rent or wages. Gasoline rose 3.9% in the month and by the BLS's own account that single line accounted for over a third of the whole increase; fuel oil jumped 10.1% in a month and is up 52.0% in a year; airline fares, which are essentially jet fuel with seats attached, rose 2.7%. Meanwhile shelter decelerated to +0.26% and core goods were nearly inert at +0.11%. So the Fed will almost certainly raise rates Wednesday - the market puts it at 87.5% - but it is raising them into a fuel shock that started reversing on Friday afternoon, not into an overheating economy.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

Start with the composition, because it is the whole story. Core CPI's tenth-of-a-point beat came from services, and within services from the two line items mechanically tied to the price of a barrel: transportation services +0.45% and airline fares +2.68%. Shelter, which has been the marginal driver of this cycle for two years, decelerated to +0.26% for the month and 3.04% over the year. Core goods were flat at +0.11%. Medical care services actually fell. There is no confirmation of broad domestic reacceleration anywhere in the detail - what there is instead is energy commodities +4.13% in a month and +27.86% over the year, and fuel oil +52.0% over the year, which is the direct mechanical footprint of a supply crisis.

Now the tape, which looks wrong until you get the order of events right. Stocks rallied about 1% Friday, the VIX fell 11.2% to 15.84, and the 30-year yield gave back a 6bp intraday spike to close 2bp lower - all on a day the hike got locked in and the 2-year rose 7bp to 4.63%. The marginal headline was not the inflation print. It was crude: Brent reversed from an intraday $110.19 to settle at $104.61, down 2.81%, after Iran announced that the foreign ministers of Iraq and other Arab states will meet Monday in Oman to discuss safe shipping through Hormuz. Equities traded the de-escalation headline, not the CPI. That reversal held even though Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline and the Houthis took Bab el-Mandeb the same day - which tells you how much of this crude price is a risk premium that a single diplomatic headline can deflate.

The curve is the cleanest read on what the bond market thinks the Fed will accomplish. The front end priced the hike while the long end did not extend: 2s10s flattened 6bp to 33bp on the day, and 2s30s narrowed from 81bp to 72bp. That is a market saying it believes the Fed will hike enough to keep the 12-month core figure - which actually fell to 2.4% from 2.5% - anchored. Two structural oddities worth noting: the 20-year at 5.38% is yielding more than the 30-year at 5.35%, persistently and not as a one-day artifact, and the 30-year has now come off its highs - at 5.35% it exactly ties the June 2007 level rather than exceeding it, which is a distinction last week's reading did not require.

The reason the Fed will hike anyway is sitting in the consumer data. Michigan sentiment fell to 47.8 preliminary from 51.7, below every estimate in the Bloomberg survey, and year-ahead inflation expectations jumped 60bp to 4.6% - the highest since June - with the long-run measure ticking up to 3.4% after three months at 3.3%. Michigan's own explanation names the cause: "With a resurgence in fuel prices and trade tensions, consumers anticipate greater pressures on their pocketbooks to come." A central bank cannot reopen Hormuz. It can refuse to let a fuel shock become an expectations problem, and that is the entire case for Wednesday.

What to tell a client who asks whether this is 2022 again: no, and the pump is where you can see it. Diesel just set a sixth consecutive record at $6.1602 and is up 66% in a year, but gasoline at $4.3104 is still 70.6c below its own 2022 record. In 2022 everything went up together. This time the shock is concentrated in distillate - the fuel that moves freight, heats homes and flies planes - because the constraint is refining and shipping geography, not aggregate demand. That is also why the relief, when it comes, shows up at the wellhead weeks before it shows up at the pump: crude fell 2.81% Friday while retail diesel rose another 10.5c the next morning. And it is why official forecasts are so far behind - the EIA's Short-Term Energy Outlook, published September 9 with its inputs frozen September 3, still assumes Brent averages $90 in the second half and retail diesel $5.07 for the full year. Diesel is already $1.09 above that forecast.

One structural point for the week ahead, and it is unusual: no Fed official can say anything before Wednesday. The communications blackout runs September 5 through September 17, verified against three regional Fed pages - the last voice out the door was Christopher Waller on September 3, making the dovish case and making it explicitly conditional, open to holding "if inflation cools." Eight days later core accelerated and expectations jumped 60bp. His condition failed, and he has no way to say so. The next scheduled Fed appearance is Michelle Bowman in London on September 18 - after the decision. Friday's repricing was pure market inference with no official guidance to anchor it, and Wednesday brings a statement, a dot plot and a press conference all at once into that vacuum.
