THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

You may see that Asian markets fell hard overnight, with Korea down more than five percent, and wonder what broke. Nothing did. Japan's long-term borrowing costs hit a thirty-year high, and that repriced long-dated bonds everywhere, including ours.

Here is what the coverage will miss. The Canada tariff that was supposed to hit at midnight got pushed three days while a deal gets papered, so the headline risk eased and long rates rose anyway. That tells you this is about the price of long-term borrowing, not the news cycle. Short-term rates actually fell overnight and gold dropped two percent, which is not what fear looks like. There is nothing for you to do today. I am watching the Fed minutes at two o'clock, because that speaks to the rate that actually prices your bonds.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

The overnight tape looks contradictory until you separate the two stories. Story one is relief: at roughly 10:15 PM ET Trump paused the 50% tariff on about $20.2 billion of Canadian goods for three days, citing a deal subject to finalization of documents. The White House proclamation moves the trigger to 12:01 AM on August 22 (White House). It is a postponement, not a cancellation, and Prime Minister Carney used exactly that word, saying substantial progress had been made but important work remained (CBC).

Story two is the bond market, and it is the one that moved portfolios. Japan's 10-year government bond yield touched 2.945%, its highest since September 1996, rising for a seventh straight session (Reuters). Germany's 30-year hit its highest since 2011 and France's 30-year a post-2008 high (CNBC). Our own 30-year sits at 5.29%, the highest since 2007. Asia took the brunt: Korea's KOSPI fell more than five percent and the Nikkei about three percent.

Here is the tell that this is not a Federal Reserve story. The front of our curve fell overnight even as the long end held at generational highs, with the 2-year down to 4.169% and the 10-year to 4.699%. September rate-hike odds actually eased to 34.1% from 36.1% yesterday (CME FedWatch). And gold fell 2.1% in Tuesday's session after its recent run. A pure fear trade does not sell gold and buy short-term Treasuries at the same time. What is repricing is the term premium, the extra yield investors demand to lend for thirty years to governments that keep issuing debt.

Tuesday's own session was a tech-led decline on Iran risk premium, with the Nasdaq 100 down 1.68% against the S&P 500's 0.69% and a Dow that lost only 0.22% (Polygon). Nvidia fell 2.34%. The data underneath was soft where it touches housing: July housing starts dropped 12.4% to 1.239 million annualized, well under the roughly 1.35 million expected, though permits rose 5.0% (Census Bureau). Industrial production rose 0.2% with capacity utilization at 76.3% (Federal Reserve G.17).

For clients: the honest framing is that a tariff deadline was deferred and a global long-duration repricing continued, and the second one matters more to a balanced portfolio than the first. Long bonds are where the pain is; short bonds and equities are absorbing it comparatively well. This is an argument for owning bonds you intend to hold to maturity rather than trading duration on headlines, and for remembering that a higher long rate is also a higher forward return on new bond money.
