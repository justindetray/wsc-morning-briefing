THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Nothing traded this weekend except Bitcoin, but the Iran story moved backward. The President publicly rejected Tehran's seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and Iran's leaders answered with harder words, though no new strikes were reported. Friday's 2% oil drop was built on deal hopes that are now off the table, so oil may open firmer Sunday night. Underneath the headlines the picture is unchanged: a good week for stocks, a hard one for long bonds, both driven by a strong economy that costs more to finance. Nothing here calls for action; Friday's jobs report matters more than any weekend quote.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls about the weekend headlines, the honest answer is that the diplomacy went backward but the market has not had a chance to react yet.

What happened: Iran offered a seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for a truce and sanctions relief. The President said plainly he was rejecting it, arguing Iran is 'losing so badly' and that 29 ships had crossed the strait the day before. Iran's President said his government no longer trusts talks, and a senior general said the strait stays closed until US economic pressure ends. Those are threats, not new attacks, and Iran's foreign minister says Tehran is still waiting for a formal reply - so the door is not fully shut.

Why it matters for portfolios: oil fell about 2% on Friday on hope for exactly this deal, with Brent settling at $104.32. When energy futures reopen at 6 PM Eastern tonight, some of that decline will probably come back. That is a gasoline and inflation story more than a stock story - it feeds into the Fed's thinking.

The bigger backdrop is unchanged from Friday. The S&P 500 rose 1.2% last week and the Nasdaq 100 3.25%, though neither is at a new high: the S&P is 0.71% below its August 13 record and the Nasdaq 100 0.40% below its September 22 record. The 30-year Treasury yield closed at 5.49%, its highest of 2026, and nearly all of the week's rise in yields was real rates, not inflation fear. Futures price about a 64% chance the Fed hikes again in October.

The advice stays steady. Bonds above 5% pay a real return again, which is a genuine opportunity for idle cash. Stocks are near their highs without euphoria, with the VIX at 14.87. What would change the view: a sustained oil spike back through Thursday's levels, a 10-year close above 5.25%, or a hot August PCE on Wednesday that pushes the market toward a third hike.
