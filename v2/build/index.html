THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Stocks are at records across the board. The S&P 500 closed at its first record in two months on Tuesday, joining the Nasdaq. The bet is on earnings: analysts expect third-quarter profits up about 30%, led by AI and energy. Long-term Treasury yields eased a little from their 2002 highs but are still above 5.25%, so bonds remain the competition. Futures are slightly lower this morning, and oil is back above $100 after attacks on Saudi Arabia. For clients, nothing here calls for changes; today's tests are the Fed minutes at 2:00 and the 10-year auction.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is records, and what they rest on.

Stocks: the S&P 500 closed at a record Tuesday, its first since August 13, and both Nasdaq indexes set records again. The rally rests on earnings: analysts expect S&P 500 profits to grow about 30% from a year earlier in the third quarter, and results start next week with the big banks. Records on earnings growth are healthier than records on hope alone.

Bonds: the 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.27% Tuesday from 5.31%, its highest close since 2002. It drifted back toward 5.3% overnight ahead of today's auction. The auctions this week show how much demand there is for US debt at these levels.

Oil: Brent fell below $98 Tuesday morning, settled at $100.58, and is above $101 this morning. Gulf exports are recovering, but Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and a storm heading for the Gulf of Mexico keep supply risk alive. Iran talks remain stuck.

Growth: the trade deficit widened to $105.6 billion in August as imports hit a record, including a 4.4% jump in capital goods. That is AI spending showing up in the data. It is a sign of strong demand, though it subtracts from GDP.

The Fed: markets put October hike odds near one in five. Vice Chair Jefferson has said another hike can wait. Today's minutes show how many officials wanted to keep raising rates.

Why it matters: when stocks hit records while bonds pay over 5%, the bar for future returns rises. That is a reason for rebalancing discipline, not for selling.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds pay more than 5% for a decade, an opportunity to lock in income, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (now 5.27%), a 30-year above 5.75% (now 5.64%), or a resumption of US strikes on Iran that sends Brent well above $105.
