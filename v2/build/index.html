THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Election Day opens with mixed signals. Overnight the US struck Iranian missile sites on Qeshm Island in what officials called a limited, defensive action, while Trump says Iran wants a deal and mediators are still shuttling. Oil gave back only a sliver of Monday's 7.6% drop, and stock futures are flat, so markets are reading this as a skirmish inside talks rather than a return to war. Apple and Palantir beat but fell on margins and growth. For clients: election nights are noisy, history says they rarely change long-run returns; stay diversified.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the short version is that three big things are happening at once, and none of them is a reason to change a long-term plan.

What changed overnight: US forces carried out limited strikes on Iranian missile sites on Qeshm Island after an Iranian missile was fired at a US aircraft. Officials stressed this was not a return to full combat. Hours earlier, President Trump posted that Iran wants a settlement as soon as possible, and Qatari mediators were back in Tehran. Iran's chief negotiator says the ball is in the US court. Oil rose less than 1% after falling more than 7% Monday, which tells you markets still lean toward diplomacy.

Earnings: Apple and Palantir both beat expectations but fell after hours, Apple on memory-chip costs squeezing margins and Palantir on slower US commercial growth. That fits the theme of the past week: investors are demanding more from AI-related winners.

Where we stand: the S&P 500 fell 0.5% Monday and is about 3.5% below its October 6 record close. Manufacturing data were strong, with the ISM index at 55.5. The 10-year Treasury yield is 4.91%.

The election: control of Congress may not be known tonight. Historically, markets have done fine under every combination of divided and unified government; the policy noise usually fades faster than the headlines suggest. The bigger near-term swing factor is Iran: reports have cited possible US strikes as early as Wednesday, while mediators push for a framework that trades an end to Hormuz attacks for lifting the US blockade.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and avoid election-night or Iran-headline trades in either direction. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Monday 4.91%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Monday 5.28%), or US strikes that resume full combat and keep Brent well above $105 (now $105.60).
