  • BRENT THROUGH $100, FIFTH STRAIGHT UP DAY. Front-month Brent trades $100.72, +2.86% on Tuesday's $97.92 ICE settlement, after Reuters clocked $100.07 at 0721 GMT — the first print above $100 since 24 July. The last close above that level was 23 July at $100.69, 33 sessions ago. The cause is specific: Houthi strikes halted operations at some Saudi energy facilities and wounded 73. The curve matters more than the level — front-month at $100.72 against January 2027 at $93.41 is roughly $7.25 of three-month backwardation. That is the market paying up hard for prompt barrels and refusing to pay for deferred ones: a physical-availability problem, not a durable repricing of oil. The timing is what makes it matter: this lands two days before August CPI and a week before the FOMC decides.
  • DIESEL SET A RECORD, TWO DAYS AFTER THE LAST ONE. AAA's page stamped today puts diesel at $5.9424 a gallon, $0.0409 above the $5.9015 record set on 7 September — a clean break, and AAA's own highest-recorded table now carries the new mark. Regular is $4.2245, up 7.3 cents on the day and $1.03 on the year, with the regular record still $5.0165 from June 2022. Diesel is the fuel that prices everything that moves: freight, rail, agriculture, construction. A record here transmits into core goods with a lag, which is precisely the channel the Fed cannot dismiss as energy noise.
  • TUESDAY WAS ROTATION, NOT DE-RISKING. The Dow fell 1.18% and the S&P 500 0.58% — both opened at their session highs and sold all day — while the Phlx Semiconductor index rose 1.30% with 21 of 30 constituents higher and Intel up 9.05%. Breadth was worse than the index: 144 advancers against 355 decliners in the S&P 500. VIX rose 8.19% and VXN 8.33%, so there was a genuine bid for protection rather than drift. Reuters framed the session as AI worries hitting software makers. Money moved from software into silicon and out of cyclicals; it did not leave.
  • THE FED CURVE HARDENED AT EVERY HORIZON. CME FedWatch, pulled directly and stamped 8 September, now prices a 59.4% chance of a hike on 16 September, 70.2% cumulative by October, 86.5% by December and 93.6% by March — with a zero probability of a cut at every meeting through March 2027. Yet Treasuries barely moved: the 10-year closed at 4.80% and the 2-year at 4.39%, both up 2bp, leaving 2s10s at 41bp, unchanged from Friday. The front end is repricing policy; the curve is not yet pricing a policy error.

THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The number that will grab a client's eye this morning is oil through $100, and for once the headline and the substance point the same way — but not for the reason they will assume. This is a physical shortage of barrels available right now, not a new higher price for oil forever, and the futures curve says so plainly: prompt Brent at $100.72 against January at $93.41. What actually reaches a client's life is the diesel record set this morning, because diesel prices freight, food and construction. The market is not panicking either — Tuesday it rotated from software into semiconductors rather than selling everything. CPI Friday, Fed next Wednesday.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

Lead with the oil, because a client has already seen it, and then immediately reframe it. Brent traded $100.72 this morning, its first print above $100 since 24 July, and the instinct is to treat a round number as a regime change. The curve says it is not. Front-month Brent is $100.72 while January 2027 Brent is $93.41 — about $7.25 of backwardation across three months. A market that pays a $7 premium for a barrel today over a barrel in January is not telling you oil is permanently more expensive; it is telling you barrels are physically hard to get right now. The cause fits that reading exactly: Houthi strikes halted operations at some Saudi energy facilities and wounded 73 people, and only six commodity vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday against a ten-day average of about twelve. Those are logistics facts with a resolution path, not a permanent revaluation. The corollary matters for how you position: if this is scarcity, the thing to watch is the backwardation flattening, not the spot price falling.

Where it does reach a client is the pump, and that is the number I would actually raise with them. Diesel set a record at $5.9424 a gallon this morning, four cents above the record set only two days earlier. Diesel is not a consumer discretionary line item; it is an input cost for every truck, train, tractor and excavator in the country, so it propagates into food, freight and construction with a lag of months. That is the mechanism by which an energy story stops being an energy story and becomes an inflation story, and it is why the Fed cannot wave it away as volatile food-and-energy noise the way the textbook says it should.

The rates market has already drawn that conclusion. FedWatch now prices a 59.4% chance of a hike next Wednesday and 86.5% cumulatively by December, with zero probability of a cut at any meeting through March 2027 — and every one of those odds rose day over day. Say the uncomfortable part plainly: the debate is no longer when the Fed cuts, it is how many times it hikes. Notice, though, what the long end did with that news — almost nothing. The 10-year rose two basis points to 4.80% and 2s10s sat at 41bp, unchanged from Friday. The bond market is repricing the next few meetings without concluding that the Fed will break something. That distinction is worth a client's time, because it is the difference between an inflation scare and a recession scare, and they are positioned very differently.

Finally, the part that should lower a client's blood pressure rather than raise it: the equity market is not de-risking, it is rotating. Tuesday the Dow fell 1.18% while semiconductors rose 1.30% with 21 of 30 constituents higher and Intel up 9.05%. Breadth was genuinely poor — 144 advancers against 355 decliners — so this was not a broad rally in disguise, but money moved between sectors rather than leaving the market. Korea told the same story overnight, with the KOSPI up 1.40% to its first close above 7,000 since July on domestic buying. The composition of this market is changing considerably faster than its level. The two things that would change my view: the Brent curve flattening back toward flat, which would say the scarcity is resolving, or Friday's CPI printing at the top of its 0.2-0.4% range, which would validate the hawkish tail rather than the base case.
