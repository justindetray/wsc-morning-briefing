THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The weekend did not change the story. Tech had its worst week since June on worries about the cost of financing AI, while the Dow rose and bonds rallied. The risk to watch when futures reopen Sunday evening is oil: ship attacks around Hormuz hit a wartime weekly high, Brent closed at $109.86, and there were no US strikes and no deal. This week's earnings from Netflix, Meta and Tesla will test whether AI spending still pays. For clients, balanced portfolios held up as designed, and long-term plans do not need to change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls today, the story is unchanged from Friday: a tech pullback inside a market that is otherwise holding together, with oil as the wild card.

Last week: the S&P 500 fell 1.95% and the Nasdaq 100 5.5%, the worst week for tech since June. The Dow rose 0.67%, and the 10-year Treasury yield fell from 5.24% to 5.09%, so bonds cushioned balanced portfolios. The S&P 500 is about 2% below its October 6 record close.

The AI question is about financing, not demand. Over the weekend the FT reported Anthropic offered a 10% coupon on $9 billion of junior debt, up from 8.5% four months ago. Money is still flowing to AI, but at a higher price, and that is what markets repriced last week.

Oil: at least 10 ships were attacked around Hormuz last week, the most since the war began, and Brent closed at $109.86. Iran says its offer to reopen the strait is on the table; Washington wants the nuclear program addressed first. Trump is talking about striking Pickaxe Mountain but has not. Higher fuel prices are feeding inflation, which is why markets price about an 85% chance of a December Fed hike.

This week: Netflix Tuesday, Meta and Tesla Wednesday, and VIX options expire Wednesday. With October options expired, markets may move more freely in both directions.

Why it matters: concentrated tech portfolios felt last week; diversified ones much less. Weeks like this are normal inside long bull markets, and selling into them has historically cost more than it saved.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and let high-quality bonds that pay about 5% do their job. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Friday 5.09%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Friday 5.43%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105. Brent is already at $109.86; the missing piece is strikes.
