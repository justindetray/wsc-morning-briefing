THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Two things are true at once. Stocks keep climbing: the Nasdaq 100 set another record Monday and futures are up again this morning. But the 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.31%, its highest close since 2002, so borrowing costs keep rising even as stocks rally. Oil is helping, now back below $100 as Gulf exports recover. For clients, this is the trade-off of a strong economy with sticky inflation: stocks are earning their keep, and bonds now pay more than they have in a generation. Nothing here calls for changes; the next test is the Fed minutes Wednesday.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is stocks and bonds telling different stories.

Stocks: the Nasdaq 100 closed at a record Monday for the second straight session, led by chip and AI companies. The S&P 500 is within half a percent of its August record. Futures are up again this morning.

Bonds: the 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.31%, the highest since May 2002. Long rates are rising faster than short ones. That pattern usually means investors want more pay to hold long bonds, because of inflation and heavy government borrowing, not because they expect the Fed to keep raising rates. Fed hike odds for October are still only about one in four.

Inflation: the service sector kept growing in September, but the prices businesses pay rose to their highest level since 2022. That is why yields have not fallen even after a weak jobs report.

Oil: Brent fell back below $100. Saudi Arabia cut its prices to Asian buyers, and Gulf exports are recovering toward prewar levels. President Trump said the Iran war is "essentially almost over," though Iran's president called talks meaningless and tankers are still being hit.

Why it matters: higher long rates raise mortgage costs and make bonds more competitive with stocks. So far, earnings optimism around AI has outweighed that pressure, but it leaves less room for disappointment.

This week: three Fed officials speak today, the minutes of the September meeting come out Wednesday, and consumer sentiment arrives Friday.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds pay more than 5% for a decade, an opportunity to lock in income, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (now 5.31%), a 30-year above 5.75% (now 5.66%), or a resumption of US strikes on Iran that sends Brent well above $105.
