CLIENT FRAME
Friday delivered the week's most important data, and it was not good. July retail sales fell 0.6% month-over-month against +0.1% expected — the largest monthly decline in 14 months and the first outright drop in nine. The control group that feeds GDP fell 0.4% versus +0.3% expected, its worst reading since January 2025, and June was revised down. The weakness was broad rather than a gasoline artifact: online retail −2.2%, autos −1.8%, electronics −0.5%. Set against July payrolls of −23,000, the read is a consumer genuinely weakening, not merely normalizing. An hour and a half later, Michigan sentiment came in at 51.0 against 55.2 in July and roughly 54.5 expected — and the internals were worse than the headline. Current conditions fell to 51.8, expectations to 50.6, and one-year inflation expectations rose to 4.3% from 4.2%, well above the 3.4% reading in February before the Iran conflict. Only 8% of consumers now expect their income to outpace inflation. That combination — falling activity with rising inflation expectations — is the uncomfortable one, and it is why this is not a straightforward case for the Fed to cut into weakness. Markets took it calmly. The S&P slipped 0.17% to 7,785.76, giving back a fraction of Thursday's record close of 7,798.99, so Thursday's high remains the record and Friday did not set a new one. The Nasdaq 100 eased 0.13% and the Dow 0.20%, while the Russell 2000 actually rose 0.51% — an odd small-cap divergence on a weak-consumer day. VIX fell to 14.25. The bond market disagreed with the growth read: the 10-year rose about 5.5bp to 4.696%, extending the message from the week's two poorly-received auctions rather than rallying on soft data. On the week the S&P added 0.36% and the Nasdaq 100 1.09%, while the Dow lost 0.56%. One correction worth making explicitly. Several wires reported that September hike odds fell to about 30.6% after the data. Our direct CME pull for the 8/14 cohort shows hold 66.9% / hike 33.1% — barely changed from Thursday's 34.1%. The direction is right but the magnitude in the press coverage is overstated; the market did not meaningfully reprice on Friday's misses. The four-day path is 49.6% hike (8/10) → 40.6% → 34.1% → 33.1%. The week ahead is unusually loaded. Monday is the reported expiry of the 60-day window in June's interim US-Iran memorandum, and there is still no confirmed extension — a third ADNOC-operated tanker was struck Friday evening. Wednesday brings Canada's 50% tariffs at 12:01am and July FOMC minutes at 2pm. And five major retailers report between Tuesday and Thursday — Home Depot, Target, Lowe's, TJX and Walmart — which is the best possible follow-up to a bad retail sales print. Jackson Hole is the week after, August 27–29, with Warsh's first keynote as Chair expected on the 28th.
