THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The bond market finally caught a break: the 10-year Treasury yield touched its highest level since 2002 on Thursday, then fell as buyers stepped in, and short-term yields dropped sharply on softer talk from Fed officials. Markets now see under a one-in-three chance of an October rate hike. Stocks edged higher Thursday and futures are up this morning, helped by oil slipping back below $100. Today's jobs report at 8:30 is the real test; a middling number keeps this calm intact, and nothing here calls for portfolio changes.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls before the jobs report, the story is a bond market that blinked, in a good way.

What happened: on Thursday morning the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.34%, the highest since 2002. Then buyers stepped in, and it closed at 5.24%. Shorter-term yields fell more, after Fed officials said they wanted more data before raising rates again. Markets now put the chance of an October hike near 30%, down from about 70% a week ago. Stocks edged higher, and futures are up this morning.

Why it matters: high yields are attracting buyers. That is how bond markets are supposed to work, and it is a useful reminder for clients that 5% yields are an opportunity, not only a threat. A client holding cash or short-term Treasuries can now lock in yields near 5% for a decade, levels most investors have not seen in their careers.

What has not changed: inflation is still too high around the world. Europe's inflation rate jumped to 3.8%, Tokyo's beat forecasts, and American factories say their input prices rose sharply in September. Oil is still near $100 with the US sending another carrier to the Gulf. So this is a pause in the bond sell-off, not an all-clear. Nike also warned that its sales will shrink this year, citing weak demand in China and tougher competition, a reminder that not every consumer brand is thriving.

Today's test: economists expect about 90,000 jobs and 4.1% unemployment. A number near that keeps the calm. A strong number would likely push yields and hike odds back up; a weak one would pull them down further, with growth worries attached.

For clients, the message holds: lock in quality income at these yields, keep equity plans in place, and do not trade the jobs report. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40%, a 30-year above 5.75%, or payrolls strong enough to lift October hike odds back above 50%.
