THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

It was a rough Friday for tech: the Nasdaq fell almost 3% and chip stocks more than 7% as Intel and Amazon revived doubts about the payoff from AI spending. Zoom out, though: the S&P 500 fell only about half a percent for the week, the Dow rose in October, and companies are still beating earnings estimates at a high rate. Next week brings the midterms, the jobs report and a possible Iran decision. For clients: stay diversified, rebalance into weakness where you are under target, and do not let one bad day for tech set the plan.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this weekend, the story is a sharp Friday tech selloff inside a much milder week, with a heavy calendar ahead.

What happened: Intel fell almost 29% after a weak forecast, and Amazon's results renewed worries about how much cash AI spending is burning. Chip stocks fell 7.2% Friday, the Nasdaq 2.9% and the S&P 500 1.8%. Gold fell more than 3% to its lowest in about a year, while oil rose about 2% to its highest settles since April. Bitcoin slid after a reported $6.3 billion theft at the Binance exchange.

Where we stand: for the week, the S&P 500 fell about 0.6% and is roughly 3% below its October 6 record close. The Nasdaq lost 5.3% in October, its worst month since March 2025, while the Dow rose for the month. Earnings are still strong overall: 82% of S&P 500 companies that have reported beat estimates. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.94%.

Iran: reports say the US told Gulf allies to prepare for possible strikes as early as November 4, the day after the midterms, while Qatar and Pakistan push talks that may bring US and Iranian officials together in Doha. Both paths are now live within days.

Why it matters: this is a market rotating away from the most expensive AI names, not a broad economic scare. Growth was 3.4% last quarter and unemployment claims are low. Diversified investors held up far better than tech-heavy ones, which is the point of diversification.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and avoid making election-week or Iran-headline trades in either direction. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Friday 4.94%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Friday 5.31%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (Friday $113.10). Brent is there; strikes are the missing piece, and the earliest reported date is now November 4.
