THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The weekend headlines are now showing up in prices. Oil is up about 4% this morning because the Iran deal that markets hoped for on Friday was publicly rejected, and gold is down 3% because higher oil means higher interest rates for longer. Stock futures are down only about half a percent, which tells you the market sees this as an inflation and rates story, not a crisis. Both sides are still talking about talks this week. Nothing here calls for action; Wednesday's inflation report and Friday's jobs report will matter more than today's oil price.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, start with what they will see on the screen: oil sharply higher, gold sharply lower, stocks slightly lower. Those three moves tell one coherent story.

Oil first. On Friday Brent fell about 2% on hopes that Iran's seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz would be accepted. Over the weekend the President rejected it, and Iran said it would not soften its terms. So this morning Brent is back at about $108, up 4%, and it has risen almost 18% this month. Diesel is at record highs because refining is short. That is what reaches household budgets and, eventually, the Fed.

The part clients rarely hear is that physical oil is flowing better than the headlines suggest. Industry tracking data show Middle East crude exports this month at their highest since the war began, with Saudi shipments more than doubling from August. Supply is still well below where it was in February, but the trend is improving, and both governments say talks could resume this week.

Gold is the tell. If investors were frightened, gold would be rising. Instead it is down 3%, because higher oil means higher inflation, higher inflation means the Fed keeps raising rates, and higher rates make an asset that pays no interest less attractive. The 30-year Treasury yield is near its highest since 2004. The market is pricing more tightening, not a panic.

Stocks are absorbing it calmly: S&P futures are down about half a percent after last week's 1.2% gain, and the S&P 500 is 0.71% below its August 13 record. The advice stays steady. Yields above 5% are a real opportunity for idle cash, and portfolios built for an inflationary, higher-rate world do not need changing because of one weekend. What would change the view: Brent holding above $110, a 10-year close above 5.25%, or a hot August PCE report on Wednesday.
