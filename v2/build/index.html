THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Oil whipsawed overnight. It fell early on Trump's plan to let Russian diesel onto world markets, then climbed back above $105 as Iran said more tankers hit mines in Hormuz and Riyadh's airport was struck a second time. Brent at $106 is now just above our $105 line, but the trigger is US strikes keeping it well above that, and that has not happened. Stock futures are flat, bond markets are closed for the holiday, and the week's real tests are the banks on Tuesday and inflation on Wednesday. For clients, no changes are needed.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is a jumpy oil market and calm stocks.

Oil: Brent fell to about $102 in Asian trading after Trump said Russia would supply diesel to world markets and claimed an "energy ceasefire" between Russia and Ukraine that neither side has confirmed. It then rebounded to about $106 after Iran's Revolutionary Guard said a third supertanker had hit a mine in Hormuz and Riyadh's airport was attacked again. A $4 swing in one night shows how headline-driven oil is right now.

Diplomacy: Iran says the US accepted some of its peace terms but called the reply inadequate. Talks are still alive, which is part of why oil keeps snapping back and forth.

Stocks: futures are about flat after giving back early gains. The S&P 500 is 0.09% below Tuesday's record close.

Bonds: the cash market is closed for Columbus Day. Friday's 10-year yield was 5.24%, down from 5.31% a week earlier, after strong demand at last week's auctions.

This week: banks report Tuesday and Wednesday, and they will show how consumers and businesses are coping with $100 oil. Wednesday's CPI is expected to show headline inflation at 3.6% and core at 2.5%. That gap says the problem is mostly energy, not a broad price spiral, which matters for the Fed on October 28.

Why it matters: higher oil works like a tax on consumers and airlines, but it helps energy producers. A diversified portfolio holds both, which is why we do not need to guess which way oil goes next. Delta cut its 2026 forecast on Friday for exactly this reason, citing a 62% jump in its fuel bill.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds pay more than 5% for a decade, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Friday 5.24%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Friday 5.60%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $106.10, with no US strikes on Iran).
