THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

If a client calls about the "Monday Iran deadline," the honest answer is that the deadline they are reading about is not real — Treasury settled that piece back in July, and there is no sanctions cliff today. What they are actually feeling is gasoline, up about 29% from a year ago. That is a real household cost, and it is not a portfolio emergency.

Meanwhile the S&P sits within a quarter of a percent of its record high, which tells you the market has spent two months absorbing this conflict without breaking. Nothing here calls for a portfolio change. What I am watching instead is Wednesday, when the Canada tariffs are scheduled to take effect and we get the Fed minutes the same afternoon.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

Two things to know before Monday. First, the Iran "Monday deadline" that has been circulating in the press does not survive contact with the primary documents. The MOU's 60-day point does land around today, but the text says the window is extendable by mutual consent and specifies no automatic trigger (CNN MOU text). More importantly, the sanctions cliff people keep pointing at — the August 21 expiry of OFAC General License X — no longer exists. Treasury revoked GL X on July 7 and replaced it with GL X1, whose wind-down ended July 17 (OFAC). The snapback already happened five weeks ago. Anyone trading a August 21 sanctions event is trading a headline that expired.

What is actually live is physical, not legal. A third ADNOC vessel was attacked in Hormuz Friday evening (gCaptain), and transits have collapsed to roughly two vessels on Friday against 130-plus a day pre-war. Overnight Trump said he intends to declare the Strait of Hormuz US territory once the conflict ends; Iran's deputy foreign minister called that "delusional" (Al Jazeera). Crude did not trade this weekend — Globex reopens 6:00 PM ET tonight — so Friday's $82.40 WTI is the last real print, and Sunday evening is the first place this gets marked.

Second, the Canada tariff deadline is Wednesday and there is still no deal. Dominic LeBlanc said Friday that Ottawa remains "quite far away from an agreement the Prime Minister can sign off on" (Reuters). Weekend talks continued and their outcome is genuinely unknown as of this writing — I am flagging that as a gap, not filling it. The scope on the table is 50% on roughly $20 billion of goods with no USMCA exemption.

For clients: the setup into this week is a market priced for calm — VIX 14.25, S&P within 0.2% of Thursday's record — sitting on top of a weakening consumer (retail sales −0.6%, Michigan 51.0), a Fed that futures now put at a 33% chance of hiking in September, and two unresolved geopolitical clocks. That is not a reason to do anything. It is a reason to expect the week to be noisier than last week was, and to have already decided what you would do if it is.
