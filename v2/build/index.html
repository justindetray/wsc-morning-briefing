THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Markets start the week calm. Futures are flat after the Nasdaq 100 closed at a record on Friday, and Asian stocks rose, with Japan's Nikkei above 70,000. The weekend brought no deal with Iran, but oil is holding near $102 because Gulf exports are recovering and governments are releasing reserves. Treasury yields eased a little overnight, and an October Fed hike now looks unlikely after Friday's weak jobs report. For clients, nothing here calls for changes; the tests this week are the services data this morning and the Fed minutes on Wednesday.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is a quiet start after a big week.

Where we are: Friday's jobs report showed just 29,000 jobs added in September, and unemployment rose to 4.2%. Markets read that as a cooling economy, which takes pressure off the Fed. The odds of an October rate hike fell to about one in five. The Nasdaq 100 closed at a record. The S&P 500 is within 1% of its August record.

The weekend: Iran said the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until its conditions are met, and President Trump said he has a decision to make. That sounds alarming, but oil barely moved. Gulf exports have been recovering toward prewar levels, the G7 is releasing 100 million barrels of reserves, and OPEC+ left its targets unchanged. Brent is about $102.70, up less than half a percent from Friday.

The bond market: the 10-year Treasury yield is about 5.26%, just below Friday's close. Long-term rates remain the main pressure point for clients, mortgages and valuations. They stayed high even after a weak jobs report, because inflation and heavy government borrowing drive them more than hiring does.

Overseas: Japan's stock market topped 70,000 for the first time since July. In Europe, worries about France's budget pushed the euro to its lowest level in 17 months, a reminder that high government debt is a global theme, not just an American one.

This week: the services surveys this morning show whether the slowdown is spreading beyond hiring. The Fed minutes on Wednesday show how many officials wanted to keep raising rates.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds now pay about 5% for a decade, an opportunity to lock in income, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40%, a 30-year above 5.75%, or a resumption of US strikes on Iran that sends Brent well above $105.
