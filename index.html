THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

This is the week the AI trade met higher rates. Wednesday's inflation report came in hot, mostly because of gasoline, and the market now worries that rising borrowing costs will squeeze the companies building AI data centers on debt. Korea fell more than 7% and Japan more than 4% overnight, and Nasdaq futures are down another 1.3%, but the Dow is nearly flat and bonds rallied: this is a tech correction, not a broad market break. For clients: diversification is doing its job, and long-term plans do not change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is a sharp pullback in AI stocks, set off by Wednesday's inflation report.

What happened: consumer prices rose 3.7% over the past year, a bit hotter than expected, and gasoline was about half the increase. Core inflation, which excludes food and energy, was 2.6%. That raised the odds of another Fed hike, and the market sold the stocks most sensitive to borrowing costs: the AI infrastructure names. CoreWeave, which borrows heavily to build data centers, fell nearly 17%, and its bonds yield about 15%. Oracle fell 8.8% and Dell 11.4%.

Overnight it spread: Korea fell more than 7%, Japan more than 4%, and Nasdaq futures are down another 1.3%.

The context: the S&P 500 is 1.28% below its October 6 record. The Nasdaq 100 is about 4% below its record, and chip stocks are about 20% below their June peak. The Dow was nearly flat Wednesday, Apple closed above $5 trillion, and TSMC just reported record profits. The demand for AI is real; the question the market is asking is who pays for it and at what interest rate.

The bond market is helping: the 10-year yield fell to about 5.14% overnight and gold rose, which is what diversification is supposed to do on a day like this.

Why it matters: portfolios concentrated in AI and chips are feeling this; balanced portfolios much less. Corrections of this kind are normal inside long bull markets, and selling into them has historically been costly. Rebalancing toward targets is the right move; abandoning the plan is not.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds pay more than 5% for a decade, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Wednesday 5.19%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Wednesday 5.52%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $104.54).
