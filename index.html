THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Monday's tech bounce was real but small, and it is already fading this morning. The bigger change is in rates and oil: the 10-year is back to 5.15%, Brent is above $111, and markets now see a December Fed hike as about 92% likely. For clients, the message is unchanged: this is a market sorting out what AI is worth while energy keeps inflation sticky, and diversified portfolios are handling it. Netflix tonight and Meta and Tesla tomorrow are the next tests, and long-term plans do not need to change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is that the tech rebound paused while oil and rates moved back up.

Monday: the S&P 500 rose 0.4% and the Nasdaq 100 1.2%, and chip stocks ended an eight-day losing streak. Amazon and Tesla led. The S&P 500 is about 1.6% below its October 6 record close. This morning futures are slightly lower, with chip stocks giving back some of Monday's gain.

Rates: the 10-year Treasury yield rose from 5.09% to 5.15%, as higher oil prices fed inflation worries and traders rebuilt bets on Fed hikes. Markets now price about a 92% chance of a December hike, up from 85% Friday. Higher yields are the main headwind for stock valuations right now.

Oil: Brent is above $111, its highest since May. Iran says it has no choice but to control Hormuz, and its navy claims a US strike on one of its bases, which a US official denied. Trump says the US could leave any time. The fog of war is thick, and the market is paying a risk premium for it.

Earnings: GM beat and announced a $20 billion buyback, Northrop raised its outlook, and Netflix reports tonight, with Meta and Tesla tomorrow. Company results so far are solid, which is the best counterweight to the AI financing worries.

Why it matters: the drivers are rates and oil, not a collapse in earnings. That is a slow-moving backdrop, not a reason to change plans. Bonds yielding over 5% for a decade are a real alternative to cash, and a balanced portfolio collects that income while it waits.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and keep bond ladders working at today's yields. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Monday 5.15%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Monday 5.48%), or confirmed US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $111.12). Brent is there; the strike claim is disputed, and confirmation is the missing piece.
