THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

We struck Iran again overnight and oil jumped more than 3%, so the fair question is whether your portfolio is in trouble. That is really two questions. Higher crude is a real cost at the pump, where gas is already near $4.08 a gallon, and worth planning around. It is not a portfolio emergency: S&P futures are down about two tenths of one percent, and gold actually FELL overnight.

That last detail is the one to hold onto. When gold falls on a night of live military escalation, the market is telling you it is worried about interest rates, not about the world ending. The safe-haven bid simply did not show up. So there is nothing you need to do today. What I am watching is Friday's jobs report and the Fed on September 16th - that is where this gets decided, not in the headlines.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

Overnight the US struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz, after US forces observed Revolutionary Guard units preparing to fire rockets carrying sea mines into the shipping lane. Iran retaliated against two US-operated airbases in Jordan; Reuters reports the incoming missiles were intercepted with no significant impact so far. Crude rose more than 3% to roughly $86 a barrel (Reuters energy desk, 0903 GMT). The important context is that this is a hope trade unwinding rather than only a new risk premium: oil fell more than 4% LAST week because Iran and Oman were reported to be negotiating a reopening of the strait, and those talks collapsed over the weekend. The market is giving back something it had already paid for.

The reason I am not treating this as a portfolio event is what did NOT happen. Gold fell about 0.6% (Reuters spot). S&P futures are down 0.19% and Nasdaq futures 0.14% (MarketWatch, 5:57 a.m. ET). The two-year Treasury yield actually eased about 2 basis points. On a night of live military escalation in the world's most important oil chokepoint, none of the classic fear trades engaged. That pattern says the market's dominant worry is still the Federal Reserve, not the Middle East - September hike odds sit near 59% on CME FedWatch and did not fade over the weekend.

Two honest caveats, because the oil price is less solid than it looks. Ole Hansen at Saxo Bank notes the commodity reaction is amplified by thin volumes, with London closed for the UK summer bank holiday. And part of the rally may be priced on an AI-generated video the President posted claiming Iran's main export terminal at Kharg Island had been destroyed - there is no military confirmation of any such attack and an Iranian official called the claim laughable. If that is in the price, a denial is a downside catalyst. Separately, China's August data cut both ways: factory activity beat at 49.8 but services fell to 49.0, the weakest reading since December 2022.

For clients: the pump matters and the portfolio does not, at least not today. If someone is anxious, the most useful thing you can show them is that gold went DOWN last night - that is the market's own verdict that this is an oil story and a rates story, not a crisis. The calendar is where the real risk sits this week: Friday's August jobs report, then CPI on September 11th, then the Fed on September 15th and 16th. Nothing that happened overnight changes a long-term allocation, and the temptation to trade a headline before three scheduled data points is exactly the impulse that costs families money.
