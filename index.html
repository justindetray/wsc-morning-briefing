THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

"Does the technology selloff mean we should change the plan?" It is a reason to check concentration, not a reason to abandon diversification. A company crisis is not the same as a broken financial plan.

Keep near-term spending money separate from investments intended for years, and avoid making a weekend decision simply to relieve headline anxiety. We are watching whether weakness spreads beyond technology and whether inflation keeps financing costs under pressure. If your cash needs or tolerance for losses have changed, that is the reason to revisit the allocation.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

The fresh Nvidia story is about governance and disclosure: Reuters published new reporting after Friday's close about what the former CEO told other company leaders. It is not proof that Nvidia knowingly violated export rules, and the report does not establish the alleged intelligence connection. Those distinctions matter when discussing a company that sits at the center of a much larger technology supply chain. (Reuters)

Friday's completed session showed concentration rather than uniform selling: the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.22% and semiconductors 3.43%, while the Dow gained 0.10%. The VIX closed at 19.30; Bitcoin's separate UTC-day close was $68,324.00. These observations do not, by themselves, establish capitulation, forced dealer selling or a yen-funded liquidation. (Polygon)

Inflation remains a separate constraint. Friday morning's October producer-price report showed a 0.3% monthly increase, down from September's revised 1.4%, but prices excluding foods, energy and trade services still rose 0.5%. Jefferson's evening speech emphasized inflation as the key policy signal. CME's November 13 cohort prices December at 94.2% for a 25bp increase and 5.8% for 50bp, not a guaranteed outcome. (BLS; Federal Reserve; CME)

For clients: use the weekend to check cash reserves and concentrated exposures, not to make a forecast from one headline. Keep diversified long-term holdings tied to the plan and rebalance only within agreed constraints. A material change in cash needs, broader deterioration beyond technology, or persistent inflation alongside tighter financing conditions would prompt a renewed risk review.
