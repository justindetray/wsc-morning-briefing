  • THE FED HIKED 25BP TO 3.75-4.00% ON A 12-0 VOTE. No dissents in either direction - the three members who dissented for a hike in July were satisfied by getting one. A unanimous hawkish committee removes the "the doves will reassert themselves" hedge from the October trade. It is the first increase since July 2023.
  • THE DOT PLOT IS THE REAL STORY, AND IT MOVED 30-50BP. The median fed funds path went to 4.1% for end-2026, 4.1% for end-2027 and 3.9% for end-2028, from 3.8 / 3.6 / 3.4 in June. The 2026 move is a level revision; the +50bp at 2027 is a persistence revision, and that is what repriced the curve. The longer-run dot also rose, to 3.2% from 3.1%, with 2027 core PCE left unchanged - meaning the committee raised its estimate of the real neutral rate. That part cannot be un-priced by a growth scare.
  • THE CURVE BEAR-FLATTENED RATHER THAN SELLING OFF IN PARALLEL. On Treasury's own curve for the 16th, the 2-year rose 7bp to 4.74% and the 10-year to 5.01% - both new 2026 highs - while the 30-year eased to 5.35%, which is still 2bp below its 2026 peak set on the 10th. Twos-tens flattened 6bp to +27bp. A hawkish dot shift that leaves the long end lower in yield and gold higher is a market conceding Fed credibility, not pricing an inflation breakout. That is the constructive version of a hawkish surprise.
  • THE STATEMENT DROPPED ITS ENERGY "SUPPLY SHOCKS" LANGUAGE, WHICH INVERTS THE OIL TRADE. Under the old framing an oil spike was a relative-price event the Fed looked through, so the reflex was to buy duration into it. Under the new framing the same move is an inflation impulse the Fed responds to. Every position built on "energy shock, therefore Fed cuts" is now on the wrong side of the reaction function - and roughly 4mb/d of Saudi pipeline capacity is still offline.
  • MARKET PRICING MOVED HAWKISH ACROSS THE BOARD, MEASURED PROPERLY. Because the target band itself moved yesterday, the only honest cross-day comparison is at fixed rate levels. On that basis the probability of at least 4.25% by the 28 October meeting went from 43% to 51%, and of at least 4.50% by January from 48% to 61% and by March from 71% to 83%. There is still zero cut probability at every meeting on the board.
  • THE BANK OF ENGLAND HELD AT 3.75% BUT SPLIT 6-3, WITH ALL THREE DISSENTS FOR A HIKE. Greene, Mann and Pill wanted 4.00%, and the committee called inflation risks tilted to the upside - one more defection makes November live. It landed a day after UK inflation reaccelerated to 3.1%, driven by motor fuels: exactly the energy-led print the Fed just decided it will not look through. Separately, and unanimously, the MPC voted to run the gilt stock to zero - but at only 20bn pounds a year of sales, well below the 50bn pace the market had assumed. Hawkish on rates, dovish on supply, so cable barely moved.
  • HONG KONG RAISED ITS BASE RATE 25BP TO 4.25%, ITS FIRST HIKE SINCE JULY 2023, mechanically tracking the Fed under the currency peg. Imported tightening into a property market that has been recovering since 2025 is why Hang Seng was the region's laggard.
  • CRUDE FELL A SECOND STRAIGHT SESSION ON UNCONFIRMED REPORTS THAT SAUDI ARABIA MAY RESTORE HALF THE PIPELINE'S CAPACITY WITHIN DAYS. Riyadh is also reported to be offering extra cargoes through Oman. Neither report is confirmed by Saudi authorities, and nothing else has changed: no restart, no new Aramco statement since the 11 September shutdown notice, Yanbu loadings still suspended. The repair estimates run from the US energy secretary's "measured in days" to Lipow's "months" - the widest credible dispersion on a 4mb/d supply variable in recent memory.
  • LENNAR CUT ITS FULL-YEAR DELIVERY TARGET TO 80-81,000 HOMES FROM 82-83,000. That, not the earnings miss, is the news: it is a volume capitulation from the builder that has deliberately run volume over margin. Gross margin rose just 20bp even though the incentive rate fell about 90bp, because the average selling price came in below guidance - the giveback shrank mostly because the list price did. With mortgage rates approaching 7%, affordability is now binding on price, not just on incentives.
  • THE NATIONAL AVERAGE DIESEL PRICE SET A RECORD AT $6.3103, confirmed against the motoring club's own highest-recorded-price table rather than inferred from a strong tape. Regular gasoline at $4.3672 is not a record - it is about 13% below the June 2022 high. A record diesel print is a freight-cost input that propagates into core goods with a lag, and it is the most legible evidence for the chair's line that too many categories are still running above 3%.

The Fed and the BoJ both tightening inside 48 hours with dollar-yen roughly flat means the market reads the differential as unchanged; the risk is a BoJ that under-delivers on guidance. THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The Fed raised interest rates yesterday for the first time in over three years, and every single member voted for it. The quarter-point move is not the story - the story is that the Fed now expects to keep rates higher for longer than it did in June, adding half a percentage point to where it thinks rates will sit at the end of 2027. Here is the encouraging part, and it is genuinely encouraging: long-term bond yields actually went down on the news, and gold went up. When a central bank surprises hawkishly and the long end rallies, the market is telling you it believes the Fed will get inflation under control. The alternative - long yields spiking on fear of entrenched inflation - is the outcome we did not get.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

The headline is simple: the Fed raised its policy rate by a quarter point to a range of 3.75-4.00%, the first increase since July 2023, on a unanimous 12-0 vote. If a client asks only one question, it will be "why are they raising rates when everyone spent two years expecting cuts?" The honest answer is that inflation has been above target for more than five years, the unemployment rate is around 4.1% and job openings and hours are rising, so the labour side of the Fed's mandate is not asking for help. The chair put it bluntly: he would be hard-pressed to call financial conditions restrictive, so the committee removed a dose of accommodation. That framing matters - it says the Fed thinks policy is still not tight, which is a different message from "we are fighting inflation with a heavy hand."

The more important development is the projections, not the rate. The Fed's own median forecast for where rates sit at the end of 2027 rose by half a percentage point, and its estimate of the long-run neutral rate went up too. Translated: this is not a one-off adjustment that gets unwound next year. It is the Fed saying the level of interest rates that the economy can sustain is simply higher than it assumed three months ago. For a client, the practical implication is that cash and short-term bonds keep paying well, and that the refinancing arithmetic on any floating-rate debt does not improve on a schedule. It also means the Fed marked growth up and unemployment down while doing this - it is a strong-economy forecast, not a recession forecast.

Now the part worth leading with, because it runs against the instinct. Yesterday should have been a bad day for bonds, and at the front end it was: the two-year yield rose to 4.74% and the ten-year to 5.01%, both the highest of the year. But the thirty-year yield actually fell, and it remains below its own peak from earlier this month. Gold rose overnight. That combination is the market expressing confidence rather than fear. Long-term yields reflect long-term inflation expectations; if investors thought the Fed was losing control, the thirty-year would be leading the move higher, not lagging it. Instead the curve flattened, which is the textbook signature of a central bank the market believes.

The second-order change is one almost nobody will mention, and it is the one worth understanding. The Fed quietly deleted the language attributing high inflation to energy supply shocks. For the last few years, an oil spike was something the Fed looked past, on the reasoning that it would hurt growth and fade on its own. That is over. With roughly 4% of global oil supply still disrupted and diesel at a record national average of $6.31 a gallon, the Fed has now said it will respond to energy-driven inflation rather than wait it out. Anyone whose mental model is "oil shock means the Fed cuts" has the sign backwards from here.

What to do about it is, as usual, less dramatic than the news. Nothing in this changes a long-term allocation. Higher-for-longer rates are a tailwind for the fixed-income side of a balanced portfolio, which is a reversal from the 2022 experience when rising rates hurt everything at once - today the starting yields are high enough to absorb it. It is a headwind for anything that depends on cheap financing, which showed up plainly in housing yesterday: a major homebuilder cut its full-year delivery target with mortgage rates approaching 7%. And note what did not happen - stocks did not break. The market's internal mix rotated, with the rate-sensitive parts lagging and semiconductors actually finishing higher on the day. That is rotation, not risk-off, and rotation is what a healthy market does when the discount rate changes.

The trigger that would change this view: the long end. As long as the thirty-year stays below its September high while the front end rises, the market is endorsing the Fed's credibility and this stays a story about the price of money rather than a story about inflation getting away. If the thirty-year starts leading yields higher - particularly on an oil headline - that is the signal that the market has stopped believing the second half of the Fed's message, and it would warrant a genuine conversation about inflation-protected exposure. Watch tomorrow too: the Bank of Japan is expected to raise rates to their highest level since 1995, and Japanese policy is the quiet anchor under global bond yields.
