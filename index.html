THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The AI scare is back, and this time it started overseas. South Korea's market fell more than 5% overnight after the Intel and Broadcom CEOs warned that financing could become a bottleneck for AI spending, and US Nasdaq futures are down about 0.6%. This is a correction inside a strong market, not a break: the S&P 500 is 0.15% below its record, and the Dow rose Monday. Today's bank earnings and Wednesday's inflation report matter more for most portfolios than one rough night in Seoul. For clients, no changes are needed.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is AI nerves, again, while the rest of the market holds up.

What happened: on Monday, the CEOs of Intel and Broadcom warned that access to capital could become a major headwind for the AI build-out, and a Chinese memory maker announced a technical advance. Chip stocks fell 2.7% in the US. Overnight that spread to Asia: Korea's market fell more than 5%, with SK Hynix down more than 10% and Samsung down 7.4%, and SoftBank, a big OpenAI investor, fell 8.5%.

The context: AI stocks have had a huge run and a lot of spending riding on them, so they are sensitive to any hint that the money could slow. This is the second scare in a week, after Thursday's report on OpenAI's revenue.

The rest of the market: the Dow rose Monday and the S&P 500 is 0.15% below its record. Analysts expect third-quarter S&P 500 profits to rise about 25%, up from 22% in July (Bloomberg Intelligence).

Oil eased to about $105 Brent. Saudi Arabia is studying a pipeline expansion to move more crude around Hormuz, which would ease the supply squeeze over time.

Today: JPMorgan, Goldman, Wells Fargo and Citi report. Then Wednesday's CPI, expected at 3.6% headline and 2.5% core.

Why it matters: concentration risk is the real lesson. Portfolios tilted heavily toward AI and chips feel these weeks much more than diversified ones. That argues for regular rebalancing, not for selling. A client who has let a few big tech winners grow into an outsized share of the account is the right person to call this week.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds pay more than 5% for a decade, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Friday 5.24%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Friday 5.60%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $104.59).
