CLIENT FRAME
Two things changed yesterday and both point the same direction. WTI settled $82.13, up 5.1%, as Strait of Hormuz reopening hopes faded — crude is up roughly 10% in a week. And a live CME FedWatch pull now shows the September FOMC hike-favored at 53.4% vs 46.6% hold. That is a correction to this brief: since Saturday we had been carrying a hold-favored ~56-60% read sourced from news characterization rather than a direct CME pull. The direct pull disagrees, and Reuters independently reported ~52% hike odds Monday. We were wrong; the live data governs. The read-through is straightforward. An oil shock is the one inflation impulse the Fed cannot dismiss as tariff noise, and it lands the day before CPI. Equities barely moved — SPX -0.06%, four and a half points off Friday's record close — but VIX rose 3.8% to 15.46 and the 10-year added 4bp to 4.699%. That is the tape hedging, not selling. Tomorrow's number is narrow and consequential. Core CPI estimates cluster at +0.20% to +0.22% MoM, and the +0.2%/+0.3% split we flagged earlier resolves as a rounding artifact rather than a real two-camp disagreement — but Wells Fargo at +0.24% and Deutsche Bank at +0.26% form a fat right tail. Prediction markets sit cooler than the sell side. With September now priced as a coin flip that leans hike, an upside core surprise has more room to move rates than a downside one has to soothe them.
