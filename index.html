THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The weekend raised the stakes on oil. Trump said the US could strike Iran as soon as Sunday or reach a major deal, and US carriers are loading heavy munitions. No strike had happened by Sunday morning, but the risk to watch when futures reopen Sunday evening is a jump in oil from Friday's $116.72. This week also brings the Fed on Wednesday and results from Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon. For clients: this is exactly why diversified portfolios hold bonds and real assets, and plans do not need to change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls today, the story is that the weekend raised the odds of either a strike or a deal, and oil will tell us which first.

What changed: Trump said Saturday the US could attack Iran as soon as Sunday, or reach a major deal. The Wall Street Journal reported a US carrier is being loaded with bunker-buster bombs. Iran sent mixed signals: one envoy offered to reopen Hormuz if the US lifts its blockade, while a Revolutionary Guard source denied it. No strike had been reported by Sunday morning.

Where we stand: last week the S&P 500 fell 0.5% and the Nasdaq 100 0.8%, while the Dow rose. Brent rose about 6% to $116.72, the highest since April. Bonds rallied, with the 10-year at 5.00%.

Two paths: a strike would likely push oil higher at first and weigh on stocks; a deal that reopens Hormuz would likely send oil sharply lower and help both stocks and bonds. Markets cannot know which, and neither can we. That is why portfolios are built for both, and why guessing the outcome with client money is a bet, not a plan. High-quality bonds have been rallying on fear and paying about 5% while they do it.

This week: the Fed decides Wednesday, with markets pricing about an 8% chance of a hike. Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon report, and GDP and PCE inflation come out Thursday.

Why it matters: geopolitical shocks have historically hit markets hard at first and faded within weeks to months, unless they cause a lasting recession.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and avoid making decisions on a weekend headline. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Friday 5.00%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Friday 5.33%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (Friday $116.72). Brent is there; a strike is the missing piece, and the weekend made it more likely.
