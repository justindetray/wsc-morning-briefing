  • HORMUZ - THE OVERNIGHT EVENT DRIVING CRUDE. Two tankers were struck by projectiles as they exited the Strait of Hormuz on Monday evening. The Sinokor-operated Senegal Prosperity was hit first by report, and the Saudi-flagged Sidr eight minutes earlier roughly 17nm east-northeast of Khasab; both incidents were confirmed by UKMTO advisories rather than by social-media claim. WTI October is +2.36% and Brent November +1.77% in response. TWO QUALIFICATIONS THAT CUT AGAINST THE BULLISH READING, both stated because the temptation is to price the headline and stop. First, attribution is contested: Iranian sources claimed the vessels struck mines, and US Central Command explicitly refuted that, stating 'This is FALSE.' Nobody credible has yet established who fired. Second, no infrastructure has been reported damaged and no closure of the strait has occurred - this is harassment of shipping, which historically produces a risk premium that decays within days unless it repeats. The tell that the market agrees: gold FELL 1.5% on the same news. A genuine Hormuz supply scare bids gold and crude together. This one did not.
  • OIL, THE UNRESOLVED CAVEAT - Carried forward for a fourth session because it has still not been settled either way, and it is the largest single uncertainty sitting under the crude price. The video posted last week claiming Kharg Island had been destroyed was AI-GENERATED. Kharg handles the overwhelming majority of Iranian crude exports, so the claim would be genuinely material if true. It has received neither US confirmation nor US denial, and no independent imagery or shipping-traffic evidence of damage has emerged in the days since. The honest position is that this is unverified in both directions. To whatever extent a Kharg premium is still embedded in the price, it is REVERSAL risk rather than supply risk - meaning the asymmetry in crude here may be to the downside on confirmation of nothing having happened.
  • JAPAN - THE LARGEST CHANGE IN THE GLOBAL RATES PICTURE OVERNIGHT, AND THE MOST UNDERPRICED. The 10-year JGB touched 3.00%, a level last seen in 1996 - thirty years. The feeds conflict on where it actually finished: one reports the 3.00% print, another puts the session high at 2.955% and the settle at 2.947%. That conflict is left UNRESOLVED here rather than rounded to the more dramatic number; 3% is best described as touched, not settled. Market-implied odds of a Bank of Japan hike at this month's meeting have moved to roughly 80-90%, far ahead of the 57% of economists surveyed in an August Reuters poll - so the market is well out in front of the forecasting community. THE REMARKABLE PART IS THE NON-REACTION IN CURRENCY. Euro-yen moved four hundredths of one percent overnight; USD/JPY is +0.19%; AUD/JPY is essentially unchanged. A thirty-year high in Japanese long yields is being read as a purely domestic rates story rather than a carry-unwind or funding event. That may well be right. It is also the single assumption in this morning's tape most likely to be tested, because the yen carry trade is the mechanism through which Japanese rates transmit into US equity, and it is currently pricing no transmission at all.
  • FEDERAL RESERVE - THE THROUGH-LINE FOR THE WHOLE TAPE. Chair Warsh spoke at the G20 finance ministers' meeting in Asheville on Monday and came closer than he previously had to acknowledging that rate HIKES may be needed. That follows his Friday speech and completes three consecutive sessions of front-end selling: the 2-year has risen each day since. CME futures put a 25bp hike at the September 16 meeting at 59% against a 41% hold, and cut probability at EXACTLY ZERO at every meeting on the visible curve through January 2027 - a state of affairs worth naming plainly, because most portfolios were built on an assumption of eventual easing. Governor Barr speaks at 9:05 a.m. ET today on the economic outlook, the first Fed voice since Warsh and 55 minutes ahead of the ISM manufacturing print. Governor Waller follows Thursday at 8:30. Note a data caveat on the probabilities: the CME cohort is Friday's, not Monday's - verified as a genuine non-publication rather than a fetch failure, and if anything the live distribution is more hawkish than the figures shown.
  • CHINA - A BEAT, AND A CONTRADICTION. The RatingDog manufacturing PMI - the survey formerly branded Caixin, renamed this year, which is worth knowing because the 'Caixin consensus' figures still circulating do not map cleanly onto it - printed 51.5 when it was released at 9:45 p.m. ET Monday, against expectations nearer 50.9 to 51.0. That is a genuine beat and the private-survey series is now firmly in expansion. It sits awkwardly against the OFFICIAL NBS readings: manufacturing at 49.8, still in contraction, and non-manufacturing at 49.0, the weakest since December 2022. The two surveys weight differently - the private series skews smaller, more export-oriented, coastal firms - so the divergence is informative rather than contradictory: it suggests external demand is holding up considerably better than domestic services activity. Asian equity did not reward it, with the Hang Seng -0.93% and Shenzhen -1.02%.
  • VENEZUELA - A PRIMARY DOCUMENT FINALLY EXISTS, AND IT CORRECTS THE PRIOR REPORTING. A White House fact sheet now sets out terms, and the counterparty is NOT Chevron as widely assumed: it names NABEP. The published terms are 100-year concessions across 17 fields holding roughly 65 billion barrels, a 35% Department of War equity stake, and 20% off-take at cost with a right of first refusal. Two specific corrections to figures that have been circulating, including in earlier editions of this brief: the 55% equity stake is UNCORROBORATED by the primary document, and the '25-year' figure referred to the royalty horizon, not the concession term. The scale is large enough to matter for the long-run crude supply picture, though nothing here affects near-dated barrels.

THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Why is my account opening lower when August was such a good month? Because two different clocks are running. August finished up about 2.6% on the S&P 500 and better than 4% on the Nasdaq 100. This morning is one pre-market session, with technology futures lower and oil higher after two tankers were struck leaving the Persian Gulf. The month is the signal. This morning is the noise.

Here is the observable fact worth holding onto. The market now prices no chance of a rate cut at any Federal Reserve meeting between now and January. That is a real change from a year ago, and it is already in the price of every bond and every stock you own - which is precisely why it is not something to act on today. Nothing in this morning's tape touches a long-term allocation. The date that matters is Friday, when the August jobs report lands.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

The hawkish repricing is the through-line. Chair Warsh's Friday speech pushed the front end of the curve higher for a third straight session, and at the G20 finance ministers' meeting on Monday he went further, coming closer than he previously had to acknowledging that rate hikes may be needed. CME's futures-implied odds put a 25bp HIKE at the September 16 meeting at 59%, with a hold at 41% - and cut probability at exactly zero at every meeting through January 2027. Governor Barr speaks at 9:05 a.m. ET on the economic outlook, 55 minutes ahead of the ISM manufacturing print, and he is the first Fed voice since Warsh.

Japan is the larger change, and it is being underpriced. The 10-year JGB touched 3.00% overnight, a level last seen in 1996 (one feed puts the settle nearer 2.947%, so treat 3% as touched rather than closed). Market-implied odds of a Bank of Japan hike this month have moved to roughly 80-90%, well ahead of the 57% of economists in an August Reuters poll. And yet the yen crosses are unchanged - euro-yen moved four hundredths of a percent. The market is treating a 30-year high in Japanese yields as a domestic story. That is the assumption most likely to be tested.

The oil and gold combination tells you what kind of tape this is. Two tankers were struck by projectiles exiting the Strait of Hormuz on Monday evening (both confirmed by UKMTO advisories), and US Central Command explicitly refuted Iranian claims that the vessels hit mines. WTI October is up about 2.4% and Brent November about 1.8%. But gold FELL about 1.5%, a second consecutive decline. Crude up, gold down, and a fresh 52-week high in the US 10-year yield is a rates-dominated tape, not a fear-dominated one. Meanwhile China's RatingDog manufacturing PMI - the survey formerly branded Caixin - printed 51.5 against expectations nearer 51.0, a beat that sits awkwardly against the official NBS reading of 49.8.

For clients: the honest summary is that rate-cut hope has been removed from the curve entirely, and rate-HIKE risk is now the live question in both the United States and Japan. That argues for being deliberate about how much interest-rate risk a portfolio carries, and against reading a single soft pre-market session as information. Friday's August payrolls report - consensus roughly +55,000 to +58,000 jobs - is the next thing that genuinely matters.
