THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Two things changed overnight. President Trump said the US will not strike Iran before the November midterms, and oil eased back to about $103. And after Thursday's AI scare, when a report on OpenAI's revenue knocked chip stocks down 3.4%, a second report said OpenAI expects $70 billion by year end, and Nasdaq futures are up. THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is a calmer Friday after a jumpy Thursday.

Iran: President Trump said the US will not attack Iran before the November 3 midterms and called talks with Tehran productive. The Pentagon is still drawing up options, and Iran is still harassing tankers in Hormuz, so the risk has been pushed back rather than removed. Oil reacted: Brent is about $103, down from $104.28 at Thursday's settle.

AI stocks: Thursday's selloff came from a report that OpenAI's annualized revenue was about $50 billion, roughly $20 billion below what had been circulating. Chip stocks fell 3.4% and the Nasdaq 100 1.4%. Overnight, a second report said OpenAI expects at least $70 billion by year end, and Nasdaq futures are up about 0.7%. The lesson is how sensitive these stocks are to a single data point after a big run. Two-thirds of the S&P 500 still rose Thursday.

Bonds: this week's 10-year and 30-year auctions both drew strong demand. The 10-year yield closed at 5.22%, down from 5.31% on Monday, and the 30-year at 5.60%. Buyers are showing up at these levels, which takes some pressure off stocks.

The Fed: markets put October hike odds near one in five and December near 85%. Jobless claims came in at 197,000, near 57-year lows, so the job market is not giving the Fed a reason to wait.

Why it matters: concentration risk in AI-linked stocks is real, and Thursday showed it. That is a reason for rebalancing discipline, not for selling.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds pay more than 5% for a decade, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (now 5.22%), a 30-year above 5.75% (now 5.60%), or actual US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now about $103, and strikes are on hold until after the midterms).
