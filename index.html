THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the headline is the election, but the market risk this week is still Iran.

What changed overnight: Democrats won control of both the Senate and the House, flipping Senate seats in states such as Texas, Ohio, Kansas and South Carolina. From January, Washington moves to divided government, which means more oversight, more gridlock, and fewer sweeping policy changes. Futures fell sharply as results came in, with Nasdaq futures down as much as 2.4%, then recovered completely; S&P 500 futures are now up about 1%. AMD helped, guiding for 80% revenue growth next year. Gold jumped almost 3% back above $4,000.

Iran: US media report that strike plans are ready as early as today, with three aircraft carriers in the region. Iran called the latest US reply not convincing, while Qatar says it is relaying proposals between the two sides in real time. Oil slipped about 1%, so traders are not pricing a big escalation yet.

Where we stand: the S&P 500 rose 0.4% Tuesday and is about 3.2% below its October 6 record close. The 10-year Treasury yield is 4.95%, and futures markets now see an 80% chance of a December rate hike, up from 73%.

Why it matters: markets have historically done well under divided government; gridlock reduces policy surprises. The real swing factor is whether strikes resume and push oil and inflation fears higher. The new Congress is not seated until January, so nothing changes in fiscal or trade policy before then; expect hearings and headlines, not new laws, in the near term.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and avoid election or Iran-headline trades in either direction. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Tuesday 4.95%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Tuesday 5.32%), or US strikes that resume full combat and keep Brent well above $105 (now $102.37).
