THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this weekend, the story is a strong week for stocks with one new Middle East risk heading into Monday.

What happened: a Houthi attack on Riyadh's main airport killed 12 people and injured 309. The Saudi-led coalition vowed a decisive response, and Trump said he would "take a look" at joining strikes on the Houthis. Separately, a US jet disabled a cargo ship trying to run the Iran blockade, and fewer ships are moving through Hormuz. Trump has said he won't strike Iran itself before the November 3 midterms, but the conflict around it is widening, not calming.

Oil: Brent settled Friday at $104.72. Hurricane Isaias still has about 69% of Gulf output shut, though crews are returning. Energy is where these risks show up first, and higher fuel costs already pushed Delta to cut its 2026 forecast on Friday.

Stocks: the S&P 500 gained 1.15% last week and finished 0.09% below Tuesday's record, even after Thursday's AI scare.

This week: September CPI on Wednesday, with consensus expecting headline inflation to rise to 3.6% while core stays near 2.5%. That gap shows the inflation problem is mostly energy, which matters for the Fed's October 28 decision. The big banks report Tuesday and Wednesday and will give an early read on how consumers and companies are holding up.

Why it matters: a balanced portfolio is built to absorb exactly this kind of weekend news. Trying to trade around headlines usually costs more than it saves, and history rewards staying invested through stretches like this.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds pay more than 5% for a decade, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (now 5.24%), a 30-year above 5.75% (now 5.60%), or wider US military action that keeps Brent well above $105 (Friday $104.72).
