THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is that earnings are doing the heavy lifting while oil stays high.

Tuesday: the Dow rose 1% and the S&P 500 0.3%, while the Nasdaq slipped. Leadership rotated toward industrials such as 3M, a sign of a broadening market rather than a narrow tech one. The S&P 500 is about 1.4% below its October 6 record close.

Overnight: SpaceX reported revenue up more than 160% and a $4.4 billion profit, driven by AI and satellite internet. Elon Musk said SpaceX and Tesla are exploring a merger. That eased worries that AI spending will not pay off, and Nasdaq futures are higher. Netflix beat on profit but cut its advertising outlook, and its stock is down about 6%.

Tonight is the key test: Meta is the first of the big AI spenders to report, and Tesla reports too. Investors will judge whether AI investment is producing returns. Big single-day moves are common after these reports, in both directions, and rarely change long-term outcomes.

Oil: Brent is about $110. Iran says Hormuz stays closed until the US accepts its terms, and mines halted three tankers on Tuesday. Talks continue through Pakistan and Qatar, but Secretary Rubio called a deal extremely difficult. High oil keeps inflation sticky, and markets price about an 88% chance of a December Fed hike.

Why it matters: corporate results have been solid, which is what ultimately drives stock prices. Bonds paying about 5% give balanced portfolios a cushion while the oil story plays out.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and avoid trading on a single earnings report. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Tuesday 5.12%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Tuesday 5.42%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $110.45). Brent is there; strikes are the missing piece.
