THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is a Fed that held but leans toward hiking, and a split verdict on Big Tech.

What changed overnight: Alphabet fell about 10% in premarket trading after revenue and search sales missed estimates and it reported negative free cash flow, while Microsoft rose almost 4% on strong cloud growth. Nasdaq futures are down about 1%, while Dow futures are up. In Iran, the Revolutionary Guard said three tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz, and AP confirmed the US has told allies to prepare for possible strikes after the November 3 midterms. At the same time, Vice President Vance reached out to Iran to revive talks. Oil slipped.

Where we stand: the S&P 500 rose 0.3% Wednesday and is about 1% below its October 6 record close. The Fed held rates at 3.75-4.00%, but two officials voted to raise, and markets put December hike odds near 85%. The 10-year Treasury yield is back near 5%.

Today: third-quarter GDP (expected near 3.2%) and September PCE inflation (core expected 3.1%) at 8:30 AM, the ECB at 9:15 AM, and Amazon and Intel after the close. ECB President Lagarde announced she will step down in February 2027.

Why it matters: the AI story has moved from everyone wins to show me the returns. Alphabet's drop came from spending running ahead of cash flow; Microsoft's gain came from cloud revenue that is already paying. Investors who own the broad index own both, which is the point of diversification.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and keep bonds working at today's yields. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Wednesday 5.05%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Wednesday 5.37%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (January Brent now $110.19). Brent is there; strikes are the missing piece, and allies are now being told to prepare for after November 3.
