THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Bitcoin recovered some ground on Saturday, but it trades on a weekend when the U.S. stock market is closed. That is a Bitcoin price, not a vote on Monday’s stock market.

Friday’s losses were real, and a weekend bounce elsewhere does not reverse them. When stocks reopen, I’ll be looking for buyers beyond the chip companies that fell hardest. That will tell us more about the health of a diversified portfolio than Sunday’s headlines can.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

Bitcoin finished Saturday at $60,088.60, up from $58,814.99 at the end of Friday’s UTC day. For someone who owns bitcoin, that is a meaningful change. For someone trying to value a U.S. stock portfolio over breakfast on Sunday, it is the wrong market.

Friday’s semiconductor index fell 10.73%, versus 3.70% for the S&P 500. The difference matters for portfolios heavy in chipmakers. But a diversified account with near-term spending covered has a different question: did the selloff change the amount of risk the family can afford, or just the price on this week’s statement?

Wednesday brings two distinct readings at the same time. The revised third-quarter GDP estimate looks backward at economic output; October’s spending and inflation figures give a more recent view of household demand and prices. Neither release is due today.

For clients: if a withdrawal is coming, check which assets were set aside to fund it. For the rest of the portfolio, watch whether Monday’s buying or selling extends beyond technology. A broad move in a full session carries more weight than a weekend price in a different asset.
