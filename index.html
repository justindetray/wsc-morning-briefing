THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

This was a rotation week, not a crash. Money came out of the AI and chip stocks that had led for months and went into health care, consumer names and bonds. The Nasdaq 100 is down about 3% since Monday and Korea had its worst week since 2008, yet the Dow is up about 1% for the week and bonds are on track for their best week since February. That is exactly what a diversified portfolio is built to do. Inflation is still running hot, so the Fed stays on alert, but long-term plans do not need to change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is a market rotating, not a market breaking.

What happened this week: worries about how AI data centers get financed, plus hot inflation reports, knocked the AI and chip stocks that led for most of the year. The Nasdaq 100 fell about 3% since Monday, chip stocks are about 21% below their June peak, and Korea, home of Samsung and SK Hynix, had its worst week since 2008.

Where the money went: health care, consumer companies and bonds. Thursday the Dow rose 550 points while Nike, Eli Lilly, Walmart and Home Depot rallied. The 10-year Treasury yield fell from 5.19% to 5.10% in a day, and bonds are headed for their best week since February. The S&P 500 is less than 1% below its October 6 record.

The inflation picture: wholesale prices rose 6.3% over the past year, the most since 2022, much of it diesel and energy from the Middle East conflict. Shoppers kept spending, with retail sales up 0.8%. That is why the Fed is still weighing another hike; the odds of an October move are about one in four, and a December hike is close to fully priced.

The Fed Chair's long view: Warsh called AI the most consequential economic change of his lifetime, positive over time but with a bumpy path. That is a fair description of this week.

Why it matters: concentrated AI and chip portfolios felt this; balanced portfolios mostly did not. Leadership rotations like this happen inside long bull markets, and the investors who get hurt are usually the ones who chase whatever just worked.

For clients: diversification is earning its keep, high-quality bonds still pay about 5% for a decade, and this is a reasonable moment to rebalance toward targets, not to abandon them. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Thursday 5.10%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Thursday 5.43%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105 (now $106.15).
