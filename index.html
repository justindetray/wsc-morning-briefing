THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

U.S. stock futures are giving back a fraction of Monday's surge, with the Nasdaq off about 1.8% before the bell even as Asian chip markets rallied overnight to catch up with Jensen Huang's return at Nvidia. After yesterday's closing bell, Hewlett Packard Enterprise beat earnings and raised its outlook yet still dropped nearly 12% in extended trading, while bond traders priced out any remaining chance of a December Fed pause.

That split tells us leadership relief is different from valuation relief. Removing an executive overhang stopped last week's forced selling, but hardware margins and higher interest rates still govern returns from here, making today's consumer confidence report and Wednesday's inflation data the real tests.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

Overnight action split cleanly by time zone. Asian semiconductor benchmarks surged Tuesday solely to catch up with Monday's U.S. session — Taiwan's TAIEX jumped 9.1%, South Korea's KOSPI gained 7.5%, and Japan's Nikkei added 4.5% coming off a holiday — after Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO Monday morning while forming independent committees on foreign-intelligence targeting and export controls (Reuters; SEC Form 8-K). In U.S. overnight trading, by contrast, futures cooled from Monday's close: Nasdaq-100 contracts slipped 1.82% to 26,319.00 and S&P 500 futures eased 0.93% to 7,187.25 as of 5:50 a.m. ET, following Monday's regular-session rebound of 6.86% in the Nasdaq-100 and 14.03% in the SOX (Quote API; Polygon).

Two post-close developments drove the U.S. pullback. First, Hewlett Packard Enterprise reported Q4 revenue up 50% to $14.5 billion and non-GAAP EPS of $1.43 with Q1 guidance of $1.30 to $1.40 — well ahead of consensus — yet shares tumbled 11.67% in premarket trade to $42.67 after Monday's 18.09% regular-session run, pulling Dell down 6.52% ahead of tonight's report and Nvidia down 5.53% (HPE release; Quote API). Second, Monday evening's CME FedWatch settlement erased the prior 10.3% probability of a December 9 hold, leaving 94.3% on a 25-basis-point hike and 5.7% on a 50-basis-point hike above the 3.75% to 4.00% target band (CME FedWatch). WTI crude edged down 0.35% to $79.89 and Brent settled near $89.19 on steadier Middle East supply (Quote API; Reuters).

Official Federal Reserve H.15 Treasury yields for November 20 — published Monday afternoon — stood at 4.63% on the 2-year and 4.92% on the 10-year (+29 bps 2s10s), before Monday's equity rebound pushed secondary-market yields back up toward 4.94% overnight (Federal Reserve H.15; Reuters). Today's calendar brings FHFA and Case-Shiller home prices at 9:00 a.m. ET, Conference Board Consumer Confidence and the Richmond Fed survey at 10:00 a.m. ET, a $70 billion 5-year Treasury note auction at 1:00 p.m. ET, and Dell, Workday, HP Inc., and Autodesk earnings after the close (FHFA; Conference Board; NY Fed; TreasuryDirect).

For clients: resist treating Asia's overnight jump as a second leg of news — Asian markets were simply pricing Monday's Nvidia announcement for the first time. HPE's post-close selloff on strong numbers and a 100% market-implied probability of a December rate hike show that investors remain demanding on AI hardware valuations when borrowing costs stay near 5%. Keep rebalancing disciplined rather than chasing gap moves; we would watch Dell's AI server margins tonight and Wednesday morning's October PCE inflation print to see whether Monday's rebound can consolidate above last week's lows.
