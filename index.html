THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

This was a tough week for technology stocks and a fine week for almost everything else. The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.5% on worries about how AI data centers get financed at today's interest rates, while the Dow rose 0.7% and bonds rallied. Oil is the new wrinkle: Brent touched $110 Friday as attacks around the Strait of Hormuz rose, and that matters more for inflation and the Fed than the AI debate does. For clients, balanced portfolios held up as designed, and long-term plans do not need to change.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this weekend, the story is a tech pullback inside a market that is otherwise holding together.

The week: the S&P 500 fell 1.95% and the Nasdaq 100 5.5%, the worst week for tech since June. The Dow rose 0.67%. The S&P 500 is about 2% below its October 6 record close, and chip stocks are about 24% below their June peak.

Why tech fell: a hot inflation report raised the odds of more Fed hikes, and investors began asking whether the companies borrowing heavily to build AI data centers can carry that debt at 5% interest rates. CoreWeave, the poster child, swung from -15% to +6% to -12% in three days. One analyst called these companies the "subprime tranche" of the AI trade. The demand for AI is not in question; the financing is.

Oil is the bigger macro story: Brent rose to $109.86 and US crude above $100 as attacks on ships around Hormuz hit a high. Higher fuel costs already showed up in this week's inflation reports: wholesale prices up 6.3% from a year ago and import prices up 8.1%. That is what keeps the Fed leaning toward a December hike, which markets price at about 85%.

What helped: bonds. The 10-year yield fell from 5.24% to 5.09% over the week, so balanced portfolios had a cushion.

Why it matters: concentrated tech portfolios felt this, diversified ones much less. Weeks like this are normal; the S&P 500 has had four down weeks in the last six and is still only about 2% from its high.

Next week: Netflix Tuesday, Meta and Tesla Wednesday, which will test the AI story directly.

For clients: stay invested, rebalance toward targets, and let high-quality bonds that pay about 5% do their job. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (Friday 5.09%), a 30-year above 5.75% (Friday 5.43%), or US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105. Brent is already there at $109.86; the missing piece is strikes, and Trump is now talking about them.
