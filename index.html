THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client calls this morning, the story is oil, and why it matters for rates.

Oil: Brent rose about 5% overnight to roughly $105. Three things hit at once. Hurricane Isaias is heading for the Gulf Coast, and producers have shut in about a quarter of Gulf crude output. Tanker attacks cut traffic through the Strait of Hormuz to the lowest in more than two months. And news reports say the White House asked the Pentagon for Iran strike options before the midterms. No decision has been reported. A storm is a short-lived supply hit; a return to strikes on Iran would be a longer one.

The Fed: Wednesday's minutes said most officials see another hike as likely appropriate by year end. Markets still put October odds below one in five, with December near 84%. Higher oil feeds inflation, which supports that path.

Bonds: the 10-year touched about 5.37% before a strong auction pulled it back to 5.28% at the close. Demand was well above average. That is a useful sign that buyers show up at these yields. The 30-year closed at 5.67%, a hair above Monday's level, ahead of today's 30-year auction.

Stocks: the S&P 500 slipped 0.2% from Tuesday's record, and futures are lower this morning. Earnings season starts next week with the banks, and analysts expect third-quarter profits up about 30%. Records built on earnings are sturdier than records built on hope.

Why it matters: oil near $105 and long yields near 5.7% are the two forces most likely to test stock valuations. Neither has broken its line yet.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds pay more than 5% for a decade, and long-term equity plans do not need changing. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40% (now 5.28%), a 30-year above 5.75% (now 5.67%), or actual US strikes on Iran that keep Brent well above $105. Brent is at that line this morning, so we're watching it, but strike options are planning, not action.
