THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The question this morning: "oil is falling hard, stocks are up, and I keep reading that the Fed might RAISE rates - which of those is right?" All of them, and they fit together. Iran and Oman agreed a framework to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, so crude has fallen three sessions and sits 6.6% below Monday's settle. Cheaper oil means cheaper inflation, which pulled bond yields down. Oil is delivering the easing the Fed will not deliver itself.

Nothing here needs a decision from you. Two things land inside the next 48 hours and both are worth knowing about rather than acting on: the Fed's preferred inflation gauge at 8:30 this morning, and Nvidia's results after the close tonight. Chair Warsh then gives his first Jackson Hole speech Friday morning. One caution on the oil story - a tanker was struck near Hormuz overnight, so the market is trading the diplomacy, not a finished peace.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

Start with oil, because everything else this morning is downstream of it. Reuters' Wednesday wrap opens with global stocks edging up because falling oil prices pushed bond yields lower on hopes the Strait of Hormuz could soon reopen. The named cause is specific: Iran and Oman unveiled a joint framework to restore safer navigation through the Strait following talks in Tehran on Tuesday, reported by both Reuters and MarketWatch and described by OCBC in the Dow Jones EMEA briefing. Brent is roughly $86 and WTI roughly $80, third consecutive declines and the lowest since Aug 13 and Aug 10 respectively. Computed over our own held price series rather than taken from a headline: Brent is down 6.61% from Monday's $92.17 settle in two sessions, WTI down 5.52%, and Brent's monthly change has now turned NEGATIVE at -2.56%. The entire war premium built through August has been handed back. The Strait carried a fifth of the world's traded oil before the war, so this is the highest-leverage price on the board.

The bond market did the arithmetic on Tuesday, not overnight. Reuters had the 10-year at 4.625% from 4.704% late Monday, a 7.9bp rally, with the 2-year down 6.0bp and the 30-year down 6.9bp, and MarketWatch called it the lowest long-end yields since Aug 5. Overnight has been a small give-back - the 10-year is 4.647%, up 1.3bp, and the 2-year 4.207%, up 2.0bp - so read Tuesday as the event and this morning as a hold. Yesterday's $69B 2-year auction is the quiet good news: it cleared at 4.204%, roughly 11bp through the July stop, and indirect bidders, largely foreign and real-money accounts, took 66.0% of competitive awards against 56.6% in July, taken from Treasury's own results file rather than a calendar aggregator. Demand for US front-end paper is not the problem some commentary implies.

Now the part that genuinely surprised me, because it runs the other way. The policy conversation turned HAWKISH inside the same overnight window. Boston Fed President Susan Collins said the Fed may need to tighten soon absent progress on inflation, published ahead of Jackson Hole. Commonwealth Bank of Australia's Kristina Clifton says markets are now pricing a Fed rate hike by December and that CBA itself expects three hikes by mid-2027 against roughly 1.5 priced. Our own CME cohort refreshed to Tuesday's settle and still shows ZERO probability of a cut at any meeting through January 2027, with a 37.9% chance of a September hike and 69.0% cumulative by December - though every one of those figures FELL versus yesterday's cohort, consistent with Tuesday's soft data. Consumer confidence printed 89.4, the lowest since January, with the expectations component down 5.8 points, and new home sales fell 10.5% to 607,000 with the median price at a four-year low on Reuters' read. So: weak consumer, hawkish Fed talk, falling oil. Clifton's other line is the one to keep - the buyback programme eases interest costs at the margin but does not change the rising stock of debt or the concerns about fiscal dominance and Fed independence.

Two events and one caution. Nvidia reports at approximately 4:20 PM with the call at 5:00, guided to $91.0B plus or minus 2% of revenue; the number that moves the stock is the Q3 guide, where Reuters says analysts look for about $104.2B and a guide below that is the stated selloff trigger. The set-up is unusual: the VIX closed at 15.45, VIX3M at 18.21, so the term structure is in normal contango, and Reuters reports options imply a SMALLER post-earnings move than prior Nvidia prints. The market is not paying much for a binary event on a $5 trillion company. Tuesday's after-close reports rehearsed the risk precisely - Intuit, Zoom and nCino all beat on revenue and were still punished for guidance and margin trajectory, while Semtech's record data-centre quarter was rewarded. The print is not the risk; the guide is. The caution: a new tanker was struck near Hormuz overnight with Iran vowing retaliation for the US economic offensive, per the Wall Street Journal, and Treasury's promised designation of a large foreign financial institution had NOT happened as of this morning. City Index's Fiona Cincotta put it well - we really need to see the traffic move through the Strait of Hormuz before getting comfortable that inflationary pressures are cooling considerably. For clients: the useful frame today is that the cheapest form of monetary easing available right now is a lower oil price, it arrived from diplomacy rather than from Washington, and it can be taken away by a single tanker - which is exactly why we do not build plans around any one of these headlines.
