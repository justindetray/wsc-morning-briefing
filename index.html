Overnight

Single names: NVDA +1.34% (Fri close); NKE -3.64% (Fri close, post-results).

  • THE JOBS REPORT CAME IN WEAK. September payrolls rose 29,000 against 90,000 expected, and July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000; July now shows a loss of 10,000 jobs (Reuters). Unemployment rose to 4.2% as 485,000 people joined the labor force. Average hourly earnings rose 0.1%, 3.0% from a year ago, below the 3.2% forecast (investingLive). Payroll growth has averaged about 51,000 a month over the past three months.
  • OCTOBER HIKE ODDS FELL TO 21%. FedWatch now puts an October 28 hike at 21.0%, from 29.9% on Thursday and about 70% a week ago; a December hike is 85.0% priced. Chicago Fed President Goolsbee called the labor market steady and inflation the more pressing problem. Vice Chair Jefferson and Governor Bowman said Thursday there was no urgency to act again (New York Times).
  • BUT LONG YIELDS ROSE ANYWAY. The 10-year traded near 5.17% right after the report, then sold off to close at 5.28% on Treasury's curve (+4bp, +11bp on the week); the 2-year closed at 4.83% (+5bp) and the 30-year at 5.63%. The 10-year TIPS yield rose 4bp to 2.92%. A weak jobs number was not enough to sustain a bond rally while inflation stays high.

The S&P 500 rose 0.73% to 7,722.72, 0.98% below its August 13 record, and the Dow gained 0.49%. For the week, the S&P fell 0.27%, the Nasdaq 100 rose 0.65% and the Dow fell 1.26%. VIX 15.31.

THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The jobs report was soft: only 29,000 jobs were added in September and unemployment rose to 4.2%. That all but took an October Fed hike off the table, and the Nasdaq 100 closed at a record. Even so, the 10-year Treasury yield finished the day higher, at 5.28%, because inflation, not jobs, is what the bond market is worried about. For clients, a cooling job market and high yields are not a crisis; they are a chance to lock in income. Nothing here calls for portfolio changes, and the next real tests are the Fed minutes Wednesday and how oil trades when futures reopen Sunday evening.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

If a client asks over the weekend, the story is that the economy is cooling, which is what the Fed wanted, and stocks liked it.

The jobs data: employers added only 29,000 jobs in September, and the two prior months were revised down by 60,000. Unemployment rose to 4.2%, partly because nearly half a million people started looking for work. Wage growth slowed to 3.0% a year. That is a softening labor market, not a collapse.

The Fed: markets now see about a 21% chance of an October rate hike, down from about 70% a week ago. Several Fed officials said this week they are in no hurry. Some investors still expect rates to stay "higher for longer" because inflation remains well above the Fed's 2% target (CNBC).

Stocks: the Nasdaq 100 closed at a record on Friday, led by chip companies after Micron's strong results. The S&P 500 is within 1% of its August record. Gains are still narrow, though: in September, the equal-weighted S&P 500 lagged the cap-weighted index by 4.6 percentage points, one of the widest gaps since 2003 (CNBC).

The puzzle clients may notice: long-term interest rates did not fall. The 10-year Treasury yield dipped right after the report, then closed higher at 5.28%. Long rates are being driven by inflation that is still running hot at home and abroad, large government borrowing, and oil near $100. The G7 agreed Friday to release 100 million barrels of reserves, which may help fuel prices, but Iran says it is preparing for more fighting.

For clients, the message holds: high-quality bonds now pay about 5% for a decade, an opportunity for income; equity plans built for the long run do not need changing because of one jobs report. What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.40%, a 30-year above 5.75%, or a resumption of US strikes on Iran that sends Brent well above $105.
