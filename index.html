THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The question this morning: "Alibaba fell 9% overnight and Nasdaq futures are down, is the AI trade breaking?" What actually happened is simpler. Alibaba sold $10.2 billion of new stock at an 8.4% discount to fund AI chips and data centres. New shares divide the same company among more owners, so the price fell by roughly the discount. That is dilution arithmetic, not a verdict on AI.

The deal drew close to three times the orders it needed, which is the opposite of a buyers' strike. S&P futures are down only 0.3%, volatility sits near 16, gold is up and oil is down 2% - an ordinary drift into a heavy week, not stress. Nothing in your plan turns on any of it, so the honest instruction is to do nothing. The dates that matter are both Wednesday: the Fed's inflation gauge at 8:30 in the morning and Nvidia's results that evening.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

The mechanics are worth understanding because they will be reported as an AI scare. Alibaba's own announcement, dated Sunday, prices 710 million new shares at HK$112.70 for HK$80 billion, closing Wednesday, with 100% of net proceeds going to what it calls full-stack AI capability. The Straits Times reports the book drew about $28 billion of orders, roughly $6 billion of it from long-only and sovereign funds including Qatar and Norway, making this the largest primary follow-on ever by a Hong Kong-listed company and the third largest globally this year behind Alphabet and Intel. Here is the part the headlines skip: a company borrowing against its own share price to buy chips is a purchase order for AI silicon, which is a demand signal for the very company reporting Wednesday. The market sold it anyway, because dilution is certain and payback is not - CNBC notes Alibaba reported a 75% profit drop days ago on the same AI spending, and management is guiding to break-even on the AI unit in two and a half to three years.

PDD's results at 6:30 this morning corroborate the theme rather than the panic. Revenue rose 8% to RMB112.4 billion, operating profit rose 8%, but non-GAAP net income fell 13% to RMB28.5 billion, with sales and marketing up to RMB29.7 billion from RMB27.2 billion and research spending up to RMB4.6 billion from RMB3.6 billion (company release). Revenue also missed the roughly $17.1 billion the street wanted, at $16.6 billion, while adjusted earnings per share beat at $2.85. Read the two together and the overnight story is that Chinese platforms are spending heavily on AI and competition, and profits are the bill. One honest gap: I could not verify a named wire source attributing the specific Nasdaq decline to any new chip-export rule, Nvidia development or analyst action. What I can observe is a pattern - Applied Optoelectronics filed a $600 million equity distribution Friday and also fell sharply pre-market. Equity issuance to fund AI is being punished this morning, and that is a positioning phenomenon, not a fundamental one.

On oil, resist the de-escalation read. Crude is down about 2% because, per Reuters, both benchmarks had risen more than 5% over the prior two weeks and traders took profits ahead of Washington's sanctions announcement - the same Reuters report says those measures could further disrupt Middle East supply. Fewer than 20 commodity vessels crossed Hormuz over the weekend and the blockades still restrict the chokepoint, with Iran granting limited transit to Iraqi tankers at Baghdad's request. That is an operational exception, not a normalisation. Two process notes: the Treasury briefing time is still not on Treasury's own schedule and Reuters copies conflict between 1:00 and 2:00 PM, and there was never an August 21 expiry of OFAC General License X, which was revoked on July 7 and superseded. Gold at $4,644.79 is up 0.8% overnight and 13.9% on the month, and is still 15.7% below its January 28 record close of $5,510.73 - verified against two years of daily data this morning rather than assumed.

The bond market is the quiet tell. The 10-year eased 1.7bp to 4.719% while the 2-year added a basis point to 4.25%, flattening 2s10s to 47bp from 50bp on Friday - a small move, but the long end catching a bid while the front end does not is a growth signal, not an inflation one. The CME cohort still reads Friday's settle: a 41.9% chance of a September hike, 73.4% by December, and zero probability of a cut at any meeting through January 2027. CME had not published a Monday update at build time, so nothing in those numbers reflects this morning. Reuters says exactly this bond-market anxiety has raised the stakes for Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole debut on Friday at 10:00, and Treasury sells $69 billion of 2-year Tuesday, $70 billion of 5-year Wednesday and $44 billion of 7-year Thursday into it. For clients: the useful frame today is that a company issuing stock to buy chips and a market selling that company are both rational, they are simply pricing different time horizons, and your portfolio is built for the longer of the two.
