THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The call this week: "Wednesday is supposed to be enormous, should I do something first?" The calendar is loaded: the Fed's inflation gauge lands Wednesday morning, Nvidia reports that afternoon, and the new Fed chair speaks Friday. But everyone can see that calendar, so it is already in the price. Trading ahead of a date the whole market has circled is how people get the direction right and still lose money.

Last week bitcoin rose 24% and gold 5% while the S&P fell 1.4%, because futures price a 42% chance of a September hike and no cut into next year, which reprices what cash earns rather than company earnings. Nothing in your plan turns on Wednesday's number, so the honest instruction is to do nothing, and I am watching the 10-year yield at 4.74%, a 20-month high; 5% would change the arithmetic on everything you own, and no single release gets it there.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

The freshest thing on the board is not a number, it is a question about Friday. Bloomberg reported Saturday evening that investors want Chair Warsh to clarify how the Fed should respond to inflation stuck above 3%, and then added that it is not entirely clear he will actually deliver that. His 10:00 AM Friday keynote is confirmed on the Fed's own calendar; what is unconfirmed is whether it contains policy content or an intellectual framework for his reforms. That matters because of a real tension: futures price a 41.9% chance he hikes in September, while Reuters commentary has noted he has pointed to balance-sheet reduction and an AI productivity boom as his preferred alternatives to raising rates. One honest caveat on that 41.9% - it is the CME futures-implied number, and other providers' September estimates run anywhere from 27% to 45% on different methodologies. The spread is the honest answer, not any single figure, and it is a good reason not to let a client anchor on one.

On bitcoin, I did the verification rather than repeat the wires. Measured Friday to Friday, last week's 24.38% gain ranks first out of all 104 weeks of daily history available, and to make sure that was not an artifact of which day you measure from, Saturday to Saturday also ranks first at 22.27%. The move is real and it is robust to the window. The flows corroborate it: The Block, citing SoSoValue, reported US spot bitcoin and ether funds took in a combined $2.6 billion for the week, $1.9 billion of it bitcoin, the strongest week since October 2025. Three things keep it in proportion. Bitcoin is still about 38% below its October 2025 record; it gave back roughly half a percent overnight to around $76,600 in thin Sunday trade; and the press cannot agree whether the prior comparable week was March 2023 or March 2024. Silver, incidentally, quietly beat gold last week, up 6.6% against 5.3%, and is up 15.5% on the month.

The oil story needs a correction to the framing everyone is using. The claim circulating is that Hormuz traffic has gone to zero. Primary data says severely constrained but not zero: Reuters, citing Kpler, counted seven commodity ships transiting Thursday, four inbound and three outbound, roughly half Wednesday's tally. The better statistic is Al Jazeera's Kpler-based count for August 1 through 19 - of 112 oil and gas vessels that transited, only 23 used a publicly declared route, meaning more than 80% went dark. That is the real picture: the traffic is happening, it just will not say who it belongs to. Iran's security chief Rezaei escalated over the weekend, threatening for the first time any neighboring country that joins the US pressure campaign, while Iran continues quietly granting transit to Iraqi tankers - the blockade is porous and the rhetoric is not. Brent settled at $94.39, up 6.6% on the week, and Secretary Bessent details the sanctions Monday at 2:00 PM, a time that comes from Reuters and still does not appear on Treasury's own schedule.

Two items to hold quietly. Overnight Ukrainian drone strikes killed at least eight people in Russia, including two children in the port town of Yeysk, and hit a warehouse belonging to the retailer Ozon (Reuters); Russia says it downed 97 drones. This is an economic-infrastructure campaign on both sides now, which is why it belongs in a markets note at all - Russian refinery capacity is being taken offline week by week, and that is oil supply. Second, the week's mechanics are unusually clean: no Fed speaker other than Warsh appears on the Board's calendar all week, so the Committee speaks with one voice, and three coupon auctions on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday will test appetite for duration at a 20-month yield high. For clients: the calendar this week is knowable and the reactions are not, which is exactly the situation in which doing nothing is a decision rather than an absence of one - and if the week goes badly, remember that higher real yields are simultaneously the thing marking your bonds down today and the thing raising the return on every dollar you invest from here.
