THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

The auction the bond market was bracing for came and went without breaking anything. The 7-year sold at 5.085%, the highest since 1993 per Dow Jones, with the thinnest demand of the year, but it tailed by less than a basis point. Stocks finished flat, and this morning yields are a touch lower and futures a touch higher. For clients, the point is that the market is absorbing supply at a higher price, not refusing it. Today's reads are durable goods at 8:30 and final Michigan sentiment at 10:00; the bigger test is August PCE on Wednesday, Sept 30.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

The one-line version for clients this morning is that Thursday was the stress test for the bond market and it passed, at a price. The $44 billion seven-year auction stopped at 5.085%, which Dow Jones calls the highest seven-year auction yield since April 1993. Treasury's own record shows a bid-to-cover of 2.42 and indirect bidders at 57.20%, the lowest of the nine regular seven-year auctions this year on both counts. But the when-issued yield was 5.078%, so the tail was about 0.7 basis points. Yesterday I said a tail above two basis points would turn this into something disorderly. It did not happen.

The curve still moved higher. Treasury's curve file has the ten-year at 5.18%, up 7 basis points, and the thirty-year at 5.47%, each a new high for 2026, while the two-year rose only 2 basis points to 4.87%. That is a steepener: the long end is demanding more compensation, which is a term-premium story rather than a Fed story. CNBC puts the ten-year's late high near 5.225%, the highest since June 2007, which is within a few basis points of the 5.25% level I flagged. This morning it has eased back to about 5.17% and, per Bloomberg, global yields are cooling with it.

Stocks shrugged. The S&P 500 slipped 0.02% and the Nasdaq 100 rose 0.03%, so the S&P is 1.22% below its August 13 record and the Nasdaq 100 is 0.82% below its September 22 record. Underneath, the AI trade split. Meta rose 4.50%, Oracle fell 3.48% after a force majeure notice on a New Mexico data center that it says remains on track, and Akamai rose about 19% after hours on an $11.6 billion, seven-year cloud deal with Anthropic. The lesson for clients is dispersion: the same theme is producing winners and losers on the same day, which argues for breadth rather than a single bet.

The Fed piece keeps drifting hawkish. CME FedWatch has a 70.9% chance of an October hike, up from 68.6%, and New York Fed President Williams called another hike this year a reasonable expectation. The household version of this story is mortgages: Freddie Mac's 30-year rate is above 7% for the first time since January 2025. For clients with cash to put to work, high-quality bonds now pay real income. For clients carrying variable-rate debt, it is a reason to look at terms.

Oil is the swing factor. Brent settled up 3.4% Thursday and is about 1% lower this morning after Reuters reported US and Iranian negotiators are exploring a phased path to reopening Hormuz. The Houthi missiles Saudi Arabia intercepted overnight are a reminder that a headline can reverse that. Gold is up about 1.1% as yields ease.

So the client framing is this. The bond market is repricing, but it is clearing: auctions are selling, stocks are flat, and volatility is low, with the VIX at 15.67. That is an orderly adjustment to higher rates, and it is an opportunity to lock in yield. What would change my view is a ten-year close above 5.25%, or a hot August PCE report on Wednesday, Sept 30, that lifts the roughly 30% odds FedWatch now gives a third hike by January. Either would turn a repricing into a problem for equity valuations.
