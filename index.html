  • ARAMCO HAS TOLD AT LEAST TWO EUROPEAN TERM REFINERIES THEY WILL GET NO CRUDE AT ALL IN OCTOBER. Poland's Orlen is named and is buying North Sea grades to replace the barrels. Three pumping stations on the East-West pipeline are damaged; Aramco is targeting a partial restart within days but full capacity in roughly six weeks, and is lifting Gulf exports via ship-to-ship transfers off Sohar, Oman. The market read this as a geographic reshuffle rather than lost volume, which is why crude fell on the news. Bloomberg-sourced; Reuters says it could not independently verify.
  • THE INDEX LEVEL ON FRIDAY WAS A CAP-WEIGHT ARTIFACT, NOT A RALLY. The S&P closed +0.17% on 25.29bn shares against a 16.19bn twenty-day average - about 1.56x normal volume - while NYSE decliners beat advancers 1.78-to-1 and new lows beat new highs 346 to 92. Quad witching does that: the cap-weighted index was carried by semiconductors alone while the median stock fell. The SOX closed +2.78% on its high; utilities, the rate-sensitive leg, fell 1.4%.
  • FRIDAY ERASED THURSDAY'S ENTIRE BOND RALLY AND THEN SOME. The 2-year jumped 9bp to 4.76%, a new outright 2026 high, while the 5-year and 10-year each rose to 4.86% and 5.01% - tying, not exceeding, the highs set on 9/16. The curve flattened to +25bp from +27bp. CME pricing moved with it: the odds of a hike by the December meeting rose to 89.5%, and the probability the band's upper bound is at or above 4.50% by January went from 56.8% to 62.9% in a single session.
  • THE WEEK'S ENTIRE YIELD MOVE WAS REAL RATES, WITH INFLATION EXPECTATIONS COMPLETELY STILL. The 10-year breakeven printed 233bp for three consecutive sessions - 9/16, 9/17 and 9/18 - while the real 10-year yield round-tripped from 2.68% to 2.61% and back to 2.68%. That is a market repricing the growth-and-policy path, not the inflation path, even with crude near $100 and AAA diesel at an all-time high of $6.4476 a gallon.
  • TAIL HEDGING GOT MORE EXPENSIVE EVEN AS SPOT VOLATILITY COLLAPSED. The VIX fell to 14.81 from 15.44 and nine-day vol dropped to 12.27 from 13.39, yet SKEW rose to 148.10 from 145.70. Investors sold near-term volatility into the expiration while paying up for crash protection. The term structure stayed steeply upward-sloping with the VIX/VIX3M ratio at 0.81.
  • THE YEN REVERSED ITS BANK OF JAPAN SELLOFF DURING THE US SESSION. The BoJ hiked to around 1.25% on a 7-2 vote and the yen initially weakened, with dollar-yen at 157.79 by Friday morning. By the Friday close it had recovered to 156.85, and euro-yen fell from 181.10 to 179.53. The funding-currency trade did not extend the way the hike-and-weaken pattern implied earlier in the day.
  • NEXT WEEK IS A MACRO VACUUM WITH A DURATION TEST SITTING IN THE MIDDLE OF IT. There is no PCE, no GDP revision, no consumer confidence and no existing-home sales - all of those fall on or after September 29. What there is: 2-year, 5-year and 7-year auctions Tuesday through Thursday with the 10-year at 5%, new home sales Thursday, durable goods Friday, a Xi-Trump White House meeting Thursday, and the first Fed speakers since the blackout lifted.
THE SHORT ANSWER · IF A CLIENT CALLS

Friday looked calm and was not. The S&P finished up 0.17%, but that was quad-witching expiration mechanics: volume ran about 1.5 times normal and more than three stocks fell for every two that rose. Semiconductors alone held the index up. Underneath, the 2-year Treasury yield hit a new high for the year at 4.76% and the 10-year is back at 5%, which is the number that actually matters for what you own. The market is no longer arguing about whether the Fed hikes again - it is arguing about how many more times. Nothing here calls for action, but the next two weeks will be noisier than the last two.

THE FULLER PICTURE · IF THEY WANT THE MECHANICS

The useful way to describe this week to a client is that three central banks tightened into an oil shock, and the bond market decided that was the story.

The Federal Reserve raised rates 25 basis points on Wednesday to 3.75-4.00% on a unanimous 12-0 vote. The Bank of Japan followed on Friday, raising to around 1.25% on a 7-2 vote. The ECB went two weeks ago. That is a coordinated turn, and it is happening while crude sits near $100 and US diesel is at an all-time high of $6.4476 a gallon - a cost that flows straight into food and freight.

What moved most was not stocks, it was the front end of the yield curve. The 2-year Treasury yield rose 9 basis points on Friday to 4.76%, its highest level of 2026, and the 10-year is back at 5.01%. Futures markets now put roughly 89% odds on at least one more hike by December. A month ago those same markets were pricing a 7% chance of an October move; a week ago it was 43%; on Friday, 55%.

Here is the part worth emphasising, because it cuts against the intuitive story. The entire move in yields this week came from real rates, not inflation expectations. The 10-year breakeven - the market's implied forecast of average inflation over the next decade - printed exactly 233 basis points on three consecutive days while the real yield swung around underneath it. Investors are not raising their inflation forecasts. They are marking up the price of money. That distinction matters: an inflation scare and a real-rate repricing call for very different portfolio responses, and this is the second one.

On equities, be careful with Friday's headline. The S&P 500 rose 0.17%, but it was quarterly expiration - about 1.56 times normal volume - and NYSE decliners outnumbered advancers 1.78 to 1, with 346 new lows against 92 new highs. Semiconductors, up 2.78%, carried the whole index; utilities fell 1.4% because they compete with a 5% Treasury. For the week, the Dow had its worst week since March and its third straight decline, the S&P its second, while the Nasdaq finished higher. That is rotation, not a broad advance, and it is exactly what you would expect when the discount rate rises.

Even the risk gauges disagreed with each other. The VIX fell to 14.81, but SKEW - the cost of crash protection - rose to 148.10. Investors sold the near-term fear and bought the tail. That is a market that is comfortable day to day and quietly uneasy about the distribution.

For the week ahead, there is very little data: no PCE, no GDP revision, no consumer confidence. The real event is supply - the Treasury sells 2-year, 5-year and 7-year notes Tuesday through Thursday with the 10-year at 5%. How those auctions are received will tell us more about the rate path than any speech. Xi Jinping visits the White House on Thursday.

The advice is unchanged and deliberately dull: this is a repricing of the cost of capital, not a credit event or a growth scare. Higher yields make the bonds you own more valuable going forward even as they mark down today, and they are the reason the median stock is lagging the index. Rebalancing into that gap is the opportunity; reacting to the headline index level is the mistake.
