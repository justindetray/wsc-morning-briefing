Run #85's celebrated Iran-Israel halt was overtaken within 24 hours by direct US-Iran kinetic exchange. Tuesday evening at 5pm ET, US Central Command began "self-defense strikes" on Iran at President Trump's direction in response to the downing of a US Army Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz Monday night. Two US officials told CBS News the targets included Iranian radar sites. CENTCOM framed the action as a "proportional response to unjustified Iranian aggression." An Iranian Shahed drone struck the Apache; both pilots were safely rescued within two hours. Trump told ABC News that "the response should be very strong, very powerful" and separately told the Wall Street Journal that the shootdown "wasnt a big deal" and the pilot is "fine." Iranian FM Araghchi alluded to the crash with a snarky social media post telling foreign troops to "leave" the region "to reduce risk": "We prefer language of diplomacy but speak other languages too." Iranian Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf: "We prefer the language of diplomacy, but we speak other languages far more fluently. Break your commitments, and we'll switch to what we speak best. You ride the horse you saddled." IRGC Aerospace Force Commander Mousavi: "Iran neither surrenders nor gets deceived. We do not trust the enemy's promises." UN Secretary-General Guterres said he was "deeply alarmed" by the escalation. Pre-strike, Trump told reporters that negotiations were in "final throes" with a deal possible in "two or three days"; Vice President Vance told USA Today he was "confident" the conflict would not become a "quagmire." Israel continues its Hezbollah operations in Lebanon: 8 killed and 32 wounded in Israeli strikes on Tyre Tuesday; the IDF said it "eliminated a terrorist" near the Lebanon border. Day 102 of the Iran war (since Feb 28). The Israel-Iran fragile halt is now overtaken by direct US-Iran exchange.

Gold reversed sharply: -2.03% to $4,174.49 per TradingEconomics 11:02 ET, reflecting position liquidation and risk-parity unwinds flowing into CPI hedges rather than the policy-positioning bid that drove Tuesday's modest +0.48%.
